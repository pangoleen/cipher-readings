# Antonio Pérez to Juan de Vargas Mexía: three letters with passages in "Cipher 4"

BnF, Espagnol 132, f.87r-88r (Madrid, 13 Sept 1578), f.157r + 158v + 159r (Madrid, 8 Dec 1578), f.179r-180r (Madrid, 26 Jan 1579).
Gallica `btv1b10032556x`, canvases 84-85, 154 and 156, 176-177.

Status of the text (read this first):

- The **plain Spanish** of all three letters is already in print: S. R. Rubino, "The Secrets of Antonio Pérez Decoded" (Ohio State
  honors thesis, 2012), Appendix I, with `[CIFRA]` in place of every cipher passage. Gachard (1875, p.420) prints one sentence of
  f.87. So the plain text here is **reproduced** (read again from the images; my readings differ in a few words).
- The **cipher passages** (in «…» below) are a **first reading with the known key**, as far as I can find. The key is not ours:
  J. P. Devos 1950 (p.422), completed by S. Tomokiyo 2020. Cabinet Noir (2 Oct 2026) published single decoded phrases from these
  three letters in its key file `cle/cipher4_codes_perez.tsv` (for example "nadie sino solo V.m.", "Garcia de Arze", "tomara S.M.
  la [Ta] que mas convenga", "Arcauti"); it published no reading of the letters.
- No decipherment is on the leaves. No margin gloss, no interlinear clear text, no clear copy on the next leaf (canvases 84-85,
  154-156, 176-178 seen). The only period gloss is on f.87r: a name struck out and replaced by the code words `tu xi`.
- Two passes for every cipher group: pass A by the solver, pass B blind (an agent with sign names and images, no key).
  Agreement: f.87 81/89 = 91.0 %, f.157 322/353 = 91.2 %, f.179 128/141 = 90.8 %. Plain text: two passes, not measured.

Marks: «…» = decoded cipher; [..] = lost in the binding (restored letters inside); (?) = doubtful; [xi], [mo] … = code word, value
not proved (table in NOTES.md). Abbreviations are opened; the manuscript spelling is kept.

## 1. f.87r-88r — Madrid, 13 September 1578

**f.87r.** Ill.e S.r — En esta respondere a tres cartas de v.m. de 4, 24 y xxvij(?) de agosto, y primero dire que me ha pesado harto
de no aver tenido lugar de escrevir largo a v.m. con estos correos passados; pero cierto no he podido mas, y bien lo creera v.m.,
pues no lo he hecho.

Tambien dire primero que me ha pesado mucho del desgraciado parto de mi señora doña Ysabel, que estava yo muy contento por el
contentamiento de v.m.; pero pues ya son de prueva entrambos, se podra remediar el daño. Doña Juana pario, pero hija, y yo estava
con desseo de recobrar a Her.do [Hernando; Gachard read "heredero"]; quiça acertaremos otra vez, aunque v.m., con ser mas viejo,
anda mas descansado.

Hizome v.m. mucha merced en avisarme de todo lo que toca a «don Alonso de Sotomayor». Yo me he governado con el como a v.m. le ha
parescido, y v.m. continue el advertirme de todo lo que le paresciere.

Al Rey mostre la carta que el [a name of about three words, struck out; above the line: «[tu] [xi]»] escrivio a v.m., y yo creo muy
bien lo que v.m. cerca desto me escrive; pero v.m. proceda como procede, que assi lo quiere el Rey, y me ha mandado que lo escriva
a v.m.

En lo de Nazareth «[null signs] y Lalain» avra visto v.m. lo que el Rey le escrive, y por alli se ha de procurar el remedio de
aquello; porque yo bien creo lo que v.m. cerca desto me escrive, de que no «ha acudido a recoge-c-me/que (?) [xi]» como deviera.

**f.87v.** Al Rey he hablado en los particulares de v.m. y en lo mal que lo haze el marques de Ayamonte, y ha me mandado que yo
hable a Garnica para que se provea de lo de Milan alguna parte a v.m.; y quiere escr[e]vir de su mano al marques el cumplimiento de
lo demas. Y tambien hablare al Rey un dia destos en lo de la consignacion, pues v.m. ha bien menester lo que su magestad [le] da y
un pedaço mas para lo que ay se gasta. Ya [a] Cañaveras ha mandado dar su magestad 200(?) ducados, y [se] despachara la cedula.

Lo de la profession se remediara, a lo que entiendo, po[rque] su magestad me ha mandado que le de una memoria dell[o], y ya lo he
hecho, para ordenarlo a don Ant[onio] de Padilla.

Al Rey he mostrado todo lo que v.m. me ha escrito «de como [xi] no quiere [mo] sino puesto de su ma[no], y lo que» le haze mal«quisto
en aquellas par[..]tes», y la poca consideracion y muestra de mala voluntad en «honrrar a Arcauti» y p[ro]curar desacreditar «[null
signs] a(?) [Te](?)». Y ha sido muy bien que v.m. me aya avisado de todo esto, porque obra en el Rey y le parece har[to] mal. Y
v.m. se assegure que me acuerdo de v.[m.] y de sus trabajos, y que desseo verle fuera de[llos], y que lo procurare, y que sea con
mucho ben[e]ficio suyo; y si yo puedo, v.m. lo ve[ra].

**f.88r.** Don Alonso de Sotomayor se despachara presto, y con el escrivire lo que mas se offresciere. Anda desseoso de sacar a luz
lo de don Gaspar, y en verdad que, si ellos lo encaminan bien, que sea muy facil. Bolviendo a los particulares de v.m., me ha dicho
el Rey, y aun escrito, que consuele y aun esfuerce a v.m. y le diga quan satisfecho esta de su servicio; y cierto esto es mucho, y
yo procurare que las obras lo muestren.

Agora andamos embaraçados en lo de Portugal, y entiendese cierto que toca el derecho y succession al Rey nuestro señor despues del
Cardenal. Nuestro Señor la Ill.e persona de v.m. guarde y prospere como yo desseo. De Madrid, xiij de Septiembre 1578. — De v.m.
[servidor], Ant. Perez.

### English

Illustrious Sir. I answer here three letters of yours, of 4, 24 and 27(?) August. First, I am very sorry that I had no time to
write to you at length by the last couriers; truly I could not, and you will believe it, since I did not.

I am also very sorry for the unlucky delivery of my lady doña Isabel; I was glad for your gladness. But as both of you are now
proved, the harm can be mended. Doña Juana has given birth, but to a girl, and I wished to get Hernando back [or: an heir]; perhaps
we shall do better another time, although you, being the older man, take it more easily.

You did me a great favour when you told me all that touches **don Alonso de Sotomayor**. I have dealt with him as you thought
best. Go on warning me of all that seems good to you.

I showed the King the letter that [**[tu] [xi]**: a name struck out and replaced by two code words; probably "the lord don Juan"]
wrote to you, and I well believe what you write to me about it. But go on as you do: the King wants it so and has ordered me to
write it to you.

In the matter of Nazareth **and Lalaing** you will have seen what the King writes to you, and the remedy must be sought that way;
for I well believe what you write to me on this, that **[xi] has not come forward to take … in (?)** as he ought.

I have spoken to the King about your private affairs and about how badly the marquis of Ayamonte behaves. He has ordered me to
speak to Garnica, so that part of the Milan money is provided to you, and he will write to the marquis in his own hand to pay the
rest. One of these days I shall also speak to the King about the assignment, because you need what His Majesty gives you and a
piece more for what is spent there. His Majesty has already ordered 200(?) ducats to be given to Cañaveras; the warrant will be sent.

The matter of the profession will be mended, as I understand, because His Majesty has ordered me to give him a note of it; I have
done so, for an order to don Antonio de Padilla.

I have shown the King all that you wrote to me **of how [xi] wants no [mo] unless he is placed by his own hand, and what** makes
him **disliked in those parts**, and the small regard and the ill will shown in **honouring Arcauti** and in trying to discredit
**[Te] (?)**. You did very well to tell me all this, because it works on the King, and he thinks very badly of it. Be sure that I
remember you and your troubles; I wish to see you out of them, I shall work for it, and to your great profit. If I can, you will see it.

Don Alonso de Sotomayor will be sent off soon, and by him I shall write what else comes up. He wishes to bring the matter of don
Gaspar to light, and truly, if they manage it well, it should be easy. To come back to your affairs: the King has told me, and
even written, to comfort and encourage you and to tell you how satisfied he is with your service. That is much, and I shall try to
make deeds show it.

We are now busy with the matter of Portugal; it is held certain that the right and succession belong to the King our lord after
the Cardinal. Madrid, 13 September 1578. Ant. Pérez.

## 2. f.157r, 158v, 159r — Madrid, 8 December 1578

The leaves are bound out of order: the text runs f.157r, then f.158v, then f.159r (f.157v is blank, f.158r shows only show-through).

**f.157r.** Ill.e S.r — Aqui respondere a todas las cartas de v.m. con que me hallo, que son de xxj y xxvij de octubre, 2, 7, 13,
14, 22 y 23 de noviembre, aunque a algunos puntos dellas ya tengo satisfecho en otras cartas mias.

El hijo de Antonio Maytin(?), aleman de Augusta, me vio luego que vino, y dixo que despues tornaria mas despacio; no lo hizo, y poco
ha que, topandole yo, me dixo que vendria a verme y traeria consigo a aquel mercader de los escritorios y reloxes. No se si lo hara.

Lo «del quitar la leche de las amas» se ha considerado y se dessearia, pero no se puede assi de golpe; pero el tiempo es gran
persona para todo, y segun «procedieren, tomara [S.M.] la [Ta] que mas convenga».

En lo del «quatrumvirato» no ay que dezir sino que todo tomara mejor termino con la nueva orden que se ha dado de que no «aya ay
Arcauti ni nadie» sino solo «[V.m.]»; lo qual se le escrive agora «al principe de Parma». Y con cessar «el manejo del dinero» en los
de ay, «cessaran todos los [no]s».

Tambien cessara agora «la nueva correspondencia que se avia començado entre Arcauti y el correo mayor», porque «con echarlos de ay,
es remediado todo».

Harto me ha pesado de que el credito que avia de embiarse a v.m. se aya detenido tanto, y yo he **(f.158v)** ydo hoy a hablar a
Garnica sobrello, y quedamos en que me embiaria uno de 2U [2,000] ducados para que vaya co[n] el correo desta noche; y despues de
comer se lo he tornado a acordar por un villete. Desseo infinito que me le embie, porque con el se ayudasse v.m. en la necess[i]dad
que representa, la qual creo yo muy bien, y la he representado a su magestad.

Lo de la provision de v.m. desseo yo encaminar ta[n] a su gusto como v.m. mismo querria, y creame que ha[go] en ello lo que puedo.
Querria concluirlo de mane[ra] que para lo passado se diesse orden para que en e[fecto](?) se pagassen en Milan, y que en lo por
venir se hi[zies]se ay la provision a v.m. de hasta 400 ducados al mes. No alçare la mano dello, y de lo que su magestad
resolvie[re] avisare a v.m.; y tengo buena esperança de que se ha de hazer.

«A Arcauti y Curiel y a los demas se les p[e]diran las [cifra]s», y crea v.m. que no se queda[n] entre renglones «los doze mill
[ho]s co[n que]» se «queria alçar Capelo».

Viose la copia de carta de«l principe de Pa[r]ma» para «esse [te]», y algo parecia tener de lo que v.m. noto en ellas.

Con la de 2 de noviembre vino la carta de Alonso Curiel sobre lo de San Thomer [Saint-Omer], y la nueva dio mu[cho] contento; y
v.m. crea que no ganaron d[e] «[null signs] n[ada] Arcauti y Capelo en despachar [sin] sabiduria de [V.m.]», porque parecio «aca
muy m[al]», y fueron avemarias que rezaron contra si «par[a la] [Ta] que se ha tomado».

**f.159r.** Su magestad vio la carta que v.m. le escrivio a 2 de noviembre «sobre lo de la liga», y dize que todavia procure v.m.
de «yr entendiendo lo que se pudiere, y si fuesse [possible] aver a las manos algunos papeles; pero que esto sea con el mayor recato
que sea [possible]».

Muy bueno fue el advertimiento que v.m. escrivio en lo de «Mos de la Mota, de que se prendasse con» encomienda o cosa semejante
antes que «con dinero puro», por todo respecto; y alla entendera v.m. lo que en conformidad desto ha ordenado en ello su magestad.

La difficultad que ay en esta corte para hallar dineros sin muy particular cedula de su magestad he dicho a Garnica, y el trabajo
que passava en hallarlos don Diego de Çuñiga, y quanto menos podra hallarlos agora v.m. Veremos si cumple lo que me ha prometido de
embiarme el credito que arriba digo, para que vaya con este correo.

En lo demas que aqui yo no digo me remito a don Alonso de Sotomayor, que, como quien ha visto lo que aqui passa y sabe lo que yo he
platicado con el sobre todo, podra dar a v.m. muy particular relacion dello. Nuestro Señor la Ill.e persona de v.m. guarde y
prospere como yo desseo. De Madrid, 8 de diziembre 1578. — De v.m., Ant. Perez.

*Postscript (lower left of f.159r; left edge in the binding; restorations after Rubino 2012):* [El cre]dito me embia [a de]zir
Garnica que no me le puede [dar] hasta mañana, y no ha pare[cido] detener este correo, importando [tan]to; pero llevarle ha otro
correo que partira luego [con] el dup[lica]do, y quiça despachare yo con el credito a alcançar este correo.

### English

Illustrious Sir. I answer here all the letters I have from you: 21 and 27 October, 2, 7, 13, 14, 22 and 23 November; some points
I have answered already in other letters.

The son of Antonio Maytin(?), a German of Augsburg, saw me when he came and said that he would come back at more leisure. He did
not. A little while ago I met him, and he said he would come to see me and bring that merchant of the writing-desks and clocks.
I do not know if he will.

**The matter of "taking the milk from the nurses"** [so the cipher reads; a figure of speech, probably for the profits of the men who
handle the money] has been considered and would be welcome, but it cannot be done at one blow. Time is a great helper in all
things, and **as they behave, His Majesty will take the [Ta: course / decision] that fits best.**

On the **"quatrumvirate"** there is no more to say than this: all will go better with the new order, **that neither Arcauti nor
anyone else be there, but only you.** This is now written **to the prince of Parma.** And when **the handling of the money** by
the men there stops, **all the [no]s will stop.**

**The new correspondence that had begun between Arcauti and the postmaster-general** will also stop now, because **with their
removal from there all is mended.**

I am very sorry that the credit for you was held back so long. Today I went to speak to Garnica about it; we agreed that he would
send me one of 2,000 ducats to go with tonight's courier, and after dinner I reminded him by a note. I wish very much that he
sends it, so that it helps you in the need that you describe; I believe it well and have laid it before His Majesty.

I wish to settle your allowance as much to your liking as you could wish, and believe me, I do what I can. I should like this end:
for the past, an order that the money be paid in Milan; for the future, an allowance paid to you there of up to 400 ducats a
month. I shall not take my hand from it, and I shall tell you what His Majesty decides; I have good hope that it will be done.

**Arcauti, Curiel and the others will be asked for their ciphers.** And believe me, **the twelve thousand [ho: ducats or escudos?]
with which Capelo meant to make off** are not left between the lines.

The copy of the letter **of the prince of Parma** for **that [te]** was seen; it seemed to have something of what you noted.

With your letter of 2 November came the letter of Alonso Curiel on the matter of Saint-Omer; the news gave great pleasure. And
believe me, **Arcauti and Capelo gained nothing by sending despatches without your knowledge**: it **looked very bad here**, and
they were Hail Marys said against themselves, **for the [Ta] that has been taken.**

His Majesty saw your letter to him of 2 November **on the matter of the league.** He says that you should still try **to learn
what you can, and, if possible, to lay hands on some papers; but with the greatest caution possible.**

Your warning in the matter of **Monsieur de La Motte** was very good: **that he be bound by** a commandery or the like rather than
**by plain money**, for every reason. You will learn there what His Majesty has ordered in line with it.

I have told Garnica how hard it is at this court to find money without a very particular warrant of His Majesty, the trouble that
don Diego de Zúñiga had, and how much less you can find it now. We shall see if he keeps his promise to send me the credit for
this courier.

For the rest I refer you to don Alonso de Sotomayor: he has seen what goes on here and knows what I have discussed with him. Madrid,
8 December 1578. Ant. Pérez. — *Postscript:* Garnica sends word that he cannot give me the credit until tomorrow. It did not seem
right to hold this courier. Another courier, who leaves at once with the duplicate, will carry it, and perhaps I shall send him
with the credit to overtake this one.

## 3. f.179r-180r — Madrid, 26 January 1579

**f.179r.** Ill.e S.r — Las cartas de v.m. a que devo respuesta son de 7, 18, 24 de diziembre y 14 de enero, y aqui satisfare a los
puntos dellas, con dezir primero que son de mucha satisfacion a nuestro amo, el qual las ve todas (digo las que conviene), y huelga
mucho con los avisos y consideraciones de v.m. en todo lo que escrive; y assi en esto no ay que dezir sino que v.m. continue en lo
que hasta aqui.

Vi la carta de Juan Ruiz de Herrera que vino con la de v.m. de 18, y me he maravillado de lo que alli se dize. Y en lo que toca a
ver libre a v.m. de tanta pesadumbre, no me descuydo, y voy mirando como se encamine que tenga v.m. ay su provision la que se
suele; y crea v.m. que no me descuydare en esto, pues nadie dessea mas el descanso de v.m. y su provecho y contentamiento que yo.

En lo del dinero que v.m. remittio aca, aun no he podido hablar a su magestad, porque ha estado algunos dias absente en San
Lorenço, y despues de venido al Pardo ha tenido un poco de romadizo; y helo dexado para dezirselo en presencia quando viniesse
aqui o me llamasse, lo qual sera presto, y entonces lo hare como conviene.

**f.179v.** Por la de xxiiij entendi la llegada ay de don Alonso de Sotomayor, y creo bien que v.m. s[e] devio holgar mucho con el
y de entender de nos[o]tros, de testigo de vista, todo lo que huviere q[ue]rido saber. Y quanto a lo que v.m. dize de que in specie
o in genere «llevo entendid[o]» algo de lo que «[V.m.] ha escrito del», dig[o] que, aunque se le dio a entender «en gene[..]ral que
se tenia alguna notici[a] de lo» que ha passado (porque assi convino), [fu]«e con tanto recato, que ni po[r] imaginacion» el pudo
entender «que hu[vi]esse salido de [V.m.]». Y con esto puede v.m. estar cierto de que ay todo recato en [sus] papeles, con yr sobre
aviso de que lo que h[uviere] «de ser [ro: para?] mi solo» venga en la «zif[ra] particular», porque el «[ti] de[s]ta(?)» tiene mucha
occupacion y no «pue[de dexar](?) de» recognoscer todas «las [carta]s»; y con esta forma no avra peligro, y de otra [ma]nera le
podria aver.

Quedo avisado del [a word struck out] «padre Avil[a]», y por lo que del v.m. me «dize, me le i[ma]gino», y me «guardare del» com[o]
v.m. me lo advierte.

Sobre el particular de v.m. me ha hab[lado] largo don Lorenço de Vargas, y entre los **(f.180r)** dos avemos conferido lo que se
ha offrecido y nos ha parecido; y a mi me parece muy justo y muy conviniente que se procure que vuestras mercedes esten juntos y no
tan divididos, por la costa y por lo demas; y assi lo tratare con su magestad lo mas presto que pudiere, y hare en ello todo lo que
v.m. sabe que procuro en sus cosas. Y ha sido muy bien escrevirle sobrello, y que le de la carta don Lorenço de Vargas, como lo
hara luego que aqui llegue.

Lo de «Garcia de Arze» es muy differente «[du] de lo que [V.m.] sospechava. [Se](?) fue» aquella arremetida y desvio de camino
que hizo a «cierta comision» que se le dio, de que otro dia dare cuenta a v.m., que agora no tengo lugar. En esta casa tenemos
salud padres y hijos, y doña Juana besa las manos de v.m. muchas vezes; cuya Ill.e persona Nuestro Señor guarde como yo desseo. De
Madrid, 26 de enero 1579. — De v.m., Ant. Perez.

### English

Illustrious Sir. The letters I owe you an answer to are of 7, 18 and 24 December and 14 January. First: they give great
satisfaction to our master, who sees them all (I mean those that it is fit he should see), and he is very pleased with your news
and your thoughts. So go on as before.

I saw the letter of Juan Ruiz de Herrera that came with yours of the 18th, and I wonder at what it says. As for freeing you from
so much trouble, I do not neglect it; I am looking for a way for you to have the usual allowance there. Nobody wishes your rest,
profit and content more than I.

About the money that you sent here I have not yet been able to speak to His Majesty. He was away some days at San Lorenzo, and
since he came to El Pardo he has had a slight cold. I left it to tell him face to face when he comes here or calls me, which will
be soon.

By yours of the 24th I learned that don Alonso de Sotomayor has arrived there. I believe you were glad of him, and glad to hear
about us from an eye-witness. As to what you say, that in particular or in general **he came away knowing** something of what
**you have written about him**: I say that, although he was given to understand **in general that there was some knowledge of
what** had happened (it had to be so), **it was done with such care that he could not even imagine that it had come from you.** So
you can be sure that your papers are kept with all care. Only take note: what is **for me alone** should come in **the private
cipher**, because **the [ti] of this one (?)** is very busy and **cannot but** look through all **the letters**. This way there
will be no danger; the other way there could be.

I am warned about **Father Ávila**; from what you **say** of him **I can picture him, and I shall be on my guard against him**, as
you advise.

Don Lorenzo de Vargas has spoken to me at length on your own business, and we two have talked over what came up. To me it seems
right and fitting that you and he should be together and not so divided, for the cost and for the rest. I shall take it up with
His Majesty as soon as I can. It was well to write to him about it; don Lorenzo de Vargas will give him the letter when he arrives.

The matter of **García de Arce** is very different **from what you suspected.** That sudden dash and turn from his road was for
**a certain commission** that was given to him; I shall tell you of it another day, for now I have no time. In this house parents
and children are well, and doña Juana kisses your hands many times. Madrid, 26 January 1579. Ant. Pérez.
