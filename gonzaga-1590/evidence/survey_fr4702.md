## 0. Main results

SEEN on the images:

1. **f. 108r is a decipherment in the clerk's working style.** It has a heading with a date in February 1590 and a
   receipt date in April 1590. It has many struck words with other words above, some underlined words, and at least
   one small figure above a word ({fig: 39} above "pretende"). Its matter is the offer of the King of Spain to the Duke.
   This is the text that the catalogue calls no. 63. The catalogue folio (105-106) does not fit: f. 105r is a
   postscript about Giulio Centurione and f. 106r is a postscript about a battle.
2. **f. 108r, lines 1-18, has the same matter, and in part the same words, as f. 97r, lines 13-27** (I compared with the
   reduced image of f. 97r: "... di Re di Spagna li servirsi di V Ecc fuori di francia ma si li valersi di operat.ne sue
   et di suoi dependentia come il s.re duca di longa villa accioche in quello regno non succede alc.o Re Eretico ...").
   The wording is not identical.
3. **ff. 98r-99v are one text, and it is not a decipherment.** It is a long letter in normal, inflected Italian (with
   French spellings), in the clerk's hand, with few corrections and no code figures. It ends on f. 99v with a closing
   formula and "Da Desiza il 19 Mar[..]" (the page edge hides the end of the month name). The writer speaks to "V.A."
   and names "s.r Car.le Scipione" two times in the third person.
4. f. 106r (clerk's hand) and f. 107r (another hand) are two short clear texts on pasted slips. Their matter fits the
   letter of ff. 98-99.
5. No other page of ff. 90-110 is a decipherment. No page has an endorsement that names the sender.

INFERRED (not written on the pages):

- The heading of f. 108r differs from that of f. 97r (19(?) February / received 2 April, against 16 February / received
  9 April "da parigi"). So f. 108r is probably the decipherment of a second letter of Cardinal Gonzaga (a duplicate by
  another route, with changed wording), not a first draft of f. 97r. The letter of 21 January says that the Cardinal
  sent two letters "di simiglianza" by two routes. If this is correct, the two decipherments give two readings of
  almost the same plain text. Check the cipher originals in fr. 4698 for a letter of 19 February 1590.
- ff. 98r-99v: letter of the Duke of Nevers, written at Decize ("Desiza"; f. 110v has "Desize mia terra"), 19 March
  1590, to a prince with the title "Altezza" (probably the Duke of Mantua). Reasons for the year: the siege of Dreux
  and Mayenne's march (before Ivry, 14 March 1590), the journey of the Patriarch (brother of the Legate) to Rome,
  "la risposta verra qua a Pasqua". The sender is not named on the pages.
- f. 106r: postscript of the same dispatch (news of the battle of Ivry, 14 March 1590).
- f. 107r: minute of a cover note to Cardinal Scipione Gonzaga ("V S Ill.ma"), of the same dispatch. It lists the same
  enclosures as f. 99v, lines 13-15. The hand is probably the Duke's own; I did not compare it with a signed autograph.
- The old numbers agree with this: f. 98r = 311, f. 99r = 312, f. 107r = 317; ff. 94r-95r = 333-334; f. 97r = 292.

## 2. Answers to the check questions

- **Does f. 98r-v continue f. 97v?** No. f. 97v ends after 12 lines ("... e debitore et Informato") and the rest of
  the page is blank. f. 98r starts a different kind of text: a normal letter, not a word-for-word gloss. It runs from
  f. 98r to f. 99v without a break (catch-lines: 98r/98v "come dovute | a S. S.ta"; 98v/99r "per dar | soccorso";
  99r/99v "Si dole del Re Cat.co che | gli havea promesso").
- **Is it the same letter of 16 February 1590?** No (see section 0, point 3). It is dated "Da Desiza il 19 Mar[..]".
- **Endorsements.** f. 95v: blank, none. f. 96r-v: none. f. 98v: none (text page). f. 99r-v: none. The only names of
  the Cardinal are in the text of f. 99v: line 13 "al s.r Car.le Scipione", line 29 "Dal S.r Car.le Scip.ne non ho ancor
  ricevu.a la risposta di q.tta proposta fattami del q.ale n'ho(?) scritto in Ziffara a V.A.". f. 98r has "[s]ecreto"
  at the top left corner. f. 110v has "s.r Scipione" three times, but that letter is of about 1576.

## 3. Transcriptions

Conventions: one manuscript line per numbered line. [struck: ...] = struck word. {above: ...} = word written above.
{fig: NN} = small figure above a word. (?) = doubt. [..] in a word = letters that I supply for an abbreviation or
for a hidden page edge. "q.to, q.ta, q.tto, q.ale" = questo, questa, quello/quanto, quale as the clerk abbreviates them.
[scudo sign] = the triangle sign for scudi. A small "m" above a number = thousands.
I did not normalise the spelling. Accents and apostrophes are almost all mine.
