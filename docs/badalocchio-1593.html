Readings of historical ciphers

Vincenzo Badalocchio to the Duke of Nevers, Rome, 9(?) January 1593: the cipher sheet read for about four fifths, with gaps

Document
Paris, BnF, ms. français 4700, f. 80r (Gallica btv1b90582908, view 92). A cipher sheet of Vincenzo Badalocchio, agent in Rome of Ludovico Gonzaga, Duke of Nevers: 18 lines, all in figures, with no decipherment. It follows his clear letter to the Duke on f. 79r-v, dated "il dì ix(?) di Gennaro: MDXCIII". The day is read with doubt.
The cipher
It is the cipher of the Duke's minute of 1 May 1593: see nevers-1593. The letters of the key are those of the minute, unchanged.
New
This is the first reading known to us; the key is the one rebuilt from the Duke's minute of 1 May 1593 (folder nevers-1593), extended from this sheet; no key sheet of this cipher is known. It is a partial reading.
State
919 groups. 894 have a value by the key; 127 of them are nulls. 617 of the 792 groups that are not nulls (78 %) stand in words that we translate, by a count by hand. Three stretches and all names are not read. One count of line 3 does not agree: with it, the total is 613 of 792 (77 %).

The key

The cipher text is a row of figures with no gaps. The cut goes from left to right. A group that starts with 1 has three figures: it is a word or a syllable from a table in the order of the alphabet. One group is the exception: 12 (ma). A figure 4, 8 or 9 takes the next figure: the pair is one letter. A figure 3, 5, 6 or 7 stands alone and is a vowel. The figures 2 and 0 stand alone and are nulls. A name is a figure 1 with a dot, and two more figures. u and v are one letter.

The cut is not free of doubt in two cases. "5 8" can be the group 58 (si), or the letter i and the head of a pair 8x: in four places we read i + 8x (Milano two times, "cosi bel-", Sicilia). "2 9" and "3 9" can be the groups 29 and 39: we read them so in two places.

Five lines, group by group

Each image shows one part of a cipher line. Under the image, each box holds one group of the settled transcription and its value by the key. CAPITALS mark a group for a word or a syllable. The line under a group of boxes gives the word, in the letters of the cipher; the word division is ours. A hyphen marks a word that goes on in the next line. A raised dot in a word is a null. A box in grey italics stands in no word that we translate: it is a gap. Its value is what the key gives, and no more. A red question mark marks a value that is only probable. An image can show a part of the next figure at its edge.

The reading in five sentences

  1. The troubles of the Spanish lands in Italy make an opening: the State of Milan is very ill content, and Navarre with two other powers (the names are not read) could hope to raise it and to take it with ease.
  2. The death of a prince (name not read; the clear letter says that the King of Spain is near death) would add to this, but the heresy of Navarre and of another power makes such enterprises hard or impossible to carry out.
  3. Navarre is not as prudent as his own good asks, because he still perseveres stubbornly in his heresy: if he had left it on the first day of his reign, he would now be at peace in France, with the hope of the monarchy that Cardano foretells, of which Badalocchio wrote in his letters of December, number 47.
  4. The Duke is on the spot and has authority, so he must make the king a good and vigorous remonstrance, with reasons beyond those of the soul: a rising in Milan with French support, and Naples and Sicily would not(?) stay in line.
  5. So fine an occasion will never come again.

Sentences 1, 2 and 4 hold gaps. Their general sense is inferred from the words around the gaps and from the clear letter. Sentence 3 holds two gaps too, and its sense is the view of the reader. The Italian with its gaps and the translation: reading.md.

The limits of each control:

  • The blind audit used a reader of the same model family as the first reader. The two can share a wrong habit. The auditor was given the cut. So the audit does not test the cut rule, and it does not test the figures. It shows that the hidden values follow from the other values and from the Italian. The ten hidden groups all stand 8 times or more.
  • The held-out passage is short, and it is not fully blind. It has 33 groups. By the first cut, "ENTE m" (two places) and "s c i u e" did not read. The passage then taught two values of the cut: 29 and 39 are groups. We used them on this sheet. The figures of the passage have one pass.
  • The halves: we made the key on the whole sheet. "Fixed in a half" means only that a clear word of that half fixes the value. Lines 1-9 cannot supply z, et, far, io, lui, dove and non. Lines 10-18 cannot supply per, ci, come, egli, fa, in, ben and fatto.
  • The order of the word table: ten of the 17 words were fixed from the sense before we saw the order (che, chi, ci, di, et, fa, far, il, la, le). The chance that ten values fall in order is 1 in 3.6 million. Seven were fixed with the order in mind (come, da, egli, giorno, in, io, lui). They are not independent.
  • The count of the connected sense is a count by hand, by the reader. Nobody else made it.
  • The scores need a table of Italian four-letter groups that is not in this repository. decode.py repeats the counts, the halves, the order of the table and the held-out passage. It does not repeat the scores.
  • "Groups with a value" (97.3 %) proves little. A shuffled key gives a value to the same groups.

What is not resolved

  1. The reading is partial. Three stretches and all names have no reading. A gap is left as a gap.
  2. The day of the date is "ix(?)". "x" (10) is possible.
  3. The number of the piece (no. 42) is inferred by a count of the bifolia.
  4. The cut is not free of doubt in two cases: "5 8", and "2 9" or "3 9". We settled six places by the sense.
  5. Some figures were settled by the sense, not by the image. Line 7: 148, where 145 is possible. Line 12: "4 6" after the frame, where a blot is joined to the 6; and a 6 where pass B saw a sign. Line 17: a 5 where pass B read "(7)". Line 18: "83 4 6" and "8 4 3", both blots. One blot of line 18 is not settled ("5 ? 6").
  6. The scan is from black-and-white film. Blots hide figures in lines 12, 17 and 18.
  7. Four values are only probable: 29 (non), 135 (ben), 154 (dove), 163 (fatto). Each has one place on this sheet.
  8. 92 = per fits "persevera". It does not fit "per si mostra" and "per i tr". Five of its seven places do not make a clear word.
  9. Fourteen groups have no value: 132, 133, 138, 145, 166, 167, 181, 194, 198, 199, 100, 41, 80 and 39. The order of the alphabet allows guesses for four of them (132 al.., 138 bene, 166 fin, 167 fosse). These guesses are not in the key.
  10. The names are not read. 117 stands three times, 112, 138 and 178 once. 178 is in a damaged place.
  11. The letter n after a null stands in five places and makes no word: "spera·n", "uo·n", "·n et", "tr·n", "un ·n".
  12. Single words: "sole·uo·n" (sollevatione?) in line 2; "ra·nb··be" in line 7, which we read as "[diside]ra[r]ebbe": the sheet has n where the sense asks for r; "rella" (quella?) and "dovere(?)" in line 10; "non(?)" in line 16; "occasi[on]e" in line 18, where one figure is a blot. Line 2 has "spera·n di far", where "sperando" is expected.
  13. Sentence 3 of the summary. The words that we read are "si mostra prudente come disidera[r]ebbe": "shows himself prudent as one would wish". The sentence holds two gaps ("c[..]sse", and the group 138). The summary takes it as a reproach, because of the words that follow. This is our view of the sense.
  14. The two numbers in a frame. Line 11: xij (12) or xxij (22). Line 12: 47, with a blotted 4.
  15. The sign & in line 3 has no value.
  16. The count of line 3 in the table of counts does not agree with the text (see "The state").
  17. The facts of standard history in the work log are from memory: the Constable of Castile at Milan, the viceroy of Sicily, the date of the abjuration. We did not check them against a book.
  18. The clear letter is one pass from tiles. Words with (?) are not sure.
  19. Pass A was not blind to the key. The two passes differ in 34 places.
  20. We did not see views 96 to 100 of the volume (ff. 83v-88). The passage of f. 83r is the only other cipher text of Badalocchio that we saw in ff. 73-83.

What is open

  • The three stretches: the end of line 3, the first half of line 17, and line 18 after "bella occasi[on]e".
  • The short gaps in lines 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 14 and 16.
  • The fourteen groups with no value, the four names, the sign &, and the n after a null.
  • The prediction of Cardano that the writer means.

What would settle it:

  • A second pass of the passage of f. 83r, and the other cipher sheets of Badalocchio: fr. 4700 views 96-100 (not seen), fr. 4718 nos. 3-5, and fr. 4700 nos. 32-35. Each new text gives more places for the word table.
  • The key sheet of the cipher that Badalocchio sent in September 1592, if it survives.
  • A colour scan of f. 80r, for the blots of lines 12, 17 and 18.
