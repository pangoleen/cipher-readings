# Cocquet to Mangot, Rome, 13 November 1616 — reading of the cipher

Source: BnF Clairambault 369, ff. 316-317 (Gallica `btv1b9000782k`, canvases 328-330). Signed "Coquet", dated
"a Rome ce 13e novembre 1616" (f. 317v). Key: BnF Français 18009, f. 156, "chiffre baillé à monsieur le marquis
de Treinel" (Gallica `btv1b90638776`, canvas 181).

Marks: **bold** = cipher, read with the key (two transcription passes). Plain = clear French on the page (one pass
only, mine). (?) = doubtful word. Spelling of the cipher is kept; u/v and i/j are not told apart by the cipher.

## Original (f. 317r, lower half)

… J'oubliois a vous dire Monseigneur que passant a l'armee (?) de dom Pietro et a Millan le bruit y couroit partout
que le Roy estoit mort. A Millan je m'enquis d'ou pouvoit provenir cela. Un marchant francois de ma cognoissance
m'asseura par[ticuliere]ment et scavoit de bon lieu
**que le duc de Montaleon auoit mande que sa maieste auoit un sy grand desuoyement hault et bas qu'il croyoit
qu'elle ne pouuoit passer**
deux heures. Et voila (?) [d'ou il] (?) estime que ce meschant discours a couru. Cest homme est dangereux et mande
force choses
**a don Pedro**
qui le poussent a faire ce qu'il fait. Vous me pardonnerez Monseigneur sy j'ose vous dire que le moing qu'y aura
moyen qu'il sache des aff[ai]res ce sera le meilleur
**car (?) quant il les a veu mal aller il a porte**
son frere (?) a la guerre a ceste heure qu'elles sont mieulx (?)
**il le dispose a la pais**
et encores a… (?) Toutes (?) ses Intentions ne tend a autre (?) but que de pouvoir
**aneantir l'auctorite du roy en ce pays.**

Notes on the cipher text
- "Montaleon": the cipher has m-o-n-t-a-[le]-o-n. This is the duke of Monteleone, ambassador of Spain in France.
- "grand desuoyement": the cipher has g-r-a-n-d-[double]-e-s-u-o-y-e-m-e-n-t. The doubling sign gives the second d.
- "qu'il" is the code 89; "que" is 86; "le", "la", "il" are the underlined signs.
- "car": the first sign of line 7 is a large Z without a bar. The key has no exact match. C (row 3) is the nearest
  form; P would give "par quant", which is not French.
- "pais" = paix. The key has a code for "paix" (79); the writer spelled the word.
- After "don Pedro" and after "pays" there are two nulls (o with two dots). The key lists them as nulls.

## English

… I forgot to tell you, my lord, that when I passed the army (?) of Don Pedro and Milan, the rumour ran everywhere
there that the King was dead. At Milan I asked where this could come from. A French merchant whom I know assured me
in particular, and knew from a good source,
**that the duke of Monteleone had written that His Majesty had so great a flux upward and downward (vomiting and
diarrhoea) that he believed that His Majesty could not last**
two hours. This is (?) where he thinks this wicked talk came from. This man is dangerous and writes many things
**to Don Pedro**
which push him to do what he does. You will forgive me, my lord, if I dare to tell you that the less he can know of
affairs, the better,
**for (?) when he saw them go badly he carried**
his brother (?) to war; now that they are better (?)
**he disposes him to peace**
and still … (?) all (?) his intentions tend to no other (?) aim than to be able
**to destroy the authority of the King in this country.**

"Don Pedro" is Don Pedro de Toledo, governor of Milan, then at war with Savoy. Louis XIII was gravely ill at the
end of October 1616; the rumour of his death in Milan fits that date.

## Not resolved

- "son frere" (clear): the reading is doubtful ("soy faire" is the other candidate). Who "il" and "le" are in the
  last sentence depends on it.
- "car" (line 7, first sign).
- The clear words "Et voila … estime", "et encores a…", "Toutes", "autre", "mieulx", "armee".
- The rest of the letter (ff. 316r-317r top, 317v) is in clear. I read it for context only; no transcription made.
