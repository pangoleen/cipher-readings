Readings of historical ciphers

Vincenzo Badalocchio to the Duke of Nevers, Rome, December 1592 to February 1593: three cipher letters read

Documents
Paris, BnF, ms. français 4700 (Gallica btv1b90582908): three letters of Vincenzo Badalocchio, agent in Rome of Ludovico Gonzaga, Duke of Nevers. f. 85r, 5 December 1592: clear words and cipher in the same lines. f. 65r, 11 December 1592: a clear letter with two short cipher runs. f. 80r, 9(?) January 1593: a sheet of 18 lines, all in figures. No page has a decipherment.
New
These are the first readings known to us of the three letters. One key reads all three. It was rebuilt from the texts; no key sheet of this cipher is known. The readings have gaps, and no name is read from the cipher.
f. 85r
The cipher parts of the letter are read, with gaps. It is the first reading known to us. The key is the key of the sheet of 9 January 1593, with a second table of words that this letter showed. 361 of the 383 cipher groups that carry text (94 %, a hand count) stand in words that we translate. All 11 cipher runs join the clear words around them. Not read: the seven name groups (five names; we give numbers, and guesses with grades), one word in line 11, one group in line 12, the verb of line 13, three short doubts, and the groups 199 and 299 (seven places). No period decipherment of this letter is known.
f. 65r
Two short cipher runs read; both complete their clear sentences; one name is not read.
f. 80r
The sheet is read for about nine tenths, with gaps. 717 of the 778 groups that carry text (92 %) stand in words that we translate, by a hand count; 708 of 778 (91 %) by the strict count of the audit.
Correction
11 October 2026. The first version of this page gave "Navara per[ò?] si mostra prudente" and translated "Navarre [however?] shows himself prudent". The right reading is "Navara non si mostra prudente". The figure 2 is not a null: it heads a second table. The summary sentence 3 was right.

The key

The cipher text is a row of figures with no gaps. A figure 4, 8 or 9 takes the next figure: the pair is one letter. A figure 3, 5, 6 or 7 stands alone and is a vowel. The figure 0 stands alone and is a null; six signs are nulls too. A group that starts with 1 has three figures: a word of table 1. A group that starts with 2 has three figures: a word of table 2. Both tables are in the order of the alphabet, and table 2 goes on where table 1 stops. A name is the dotted stroke and three figures 1xy; the key has no value for any name. u and v are one letter.

What the cut does not settle:

  • The lone 2. In six places a 2 cannot head a group of the tables: f. 85r lines 13 and 22; f. 80r line 7 (three times) and line 18. The rule takes a lone 2 there. It can be a null, a group with no value, or a slip of the pen. This is open.
  • The cut is not the only one by form. The three letters have 119 groups "2xy" with a value. At 74 of them the cut "2 = null, then letters" leaves no new group without a value. The sense settles these places: the old gaps close, and the measure of sense rises on each letter (see "The controls", point 5).
  • 199 and 299 stand alone between clear words on f. 85r. They are groups. A null is our best guess for their value. It is not proved. We print them as groups with no value.

The readings, in short

f. 85r, 5 December 1592. Cardinal Sforza is expected in Rome. He comes to treat what [name 143] was to treat, at the instance of [name 124]. Spain is against the business. The Spaniards want [name 195] as Queen of France and say that the crown belongs to her. The plan is to make [name 128] King of France; the verb of this sentence is not read. On 30 November the Pope told Badalocchio that he much desires that the Duke withdraw from Navarre. Badalocchio answered with the wrongs done to the Duke by the other side. They spoke of a way that Capilupi proposed.

f. 65r, 11 December 1592. Badalocchio is busy: he writes down all that passed between him and [name 133] on the Duke's account. He sends it with this letter, because it seems of great weight for the Duke's service.

f. 80r, 9(?) January 1593.

  1. The troubles of the Spanish lands in Italy make an opening. If Navarre and [name 176] were [..], they could hope to raise the State of Milan, very ill content with Spain, and to take it with ease. [Name 176] has an army that is much feared.
  2. The death of [name 122] would add to this. But the heresy of the King and of [name 176] makes such fine enterprises not only hard but impossible.
  3. Navarre does not show himself as prudent as his own good asks, because he still perseveres stubbornly in his heresy, to the fourth year. If he had left it on the first day of his reign, he would be not only a king at peace in France: there would be hope of the monarchy that Cardano foretells, of which Badalocchio wrote in his letters of December, number 47.
  4. The Duke is on the spot and has authority. So he must make the king a good and vigorous remonstrance, with reasons beyond the loss of the soul: with a rising in Milan and the support of a King of France of such worth, Naples and Sicily would not stay in line,
  5. for the ill treatment by Spain. God knows when so fine an occasion will come again.

Each control has its number and its limit.

  1. The held-out test. The key and the cut of our first page were made on f. 80r alone. We applied them, with no change, to f. 85r, which they had not seen. 534 of the 569 groups (93.8 %) have a value. 313 of the 402 groups that are not nulls (77.9 %) already have the value and the place that the final reading keeps in a word that we translate. For f. 65r: 43 of 45 groups, and 27 of 39 (69.2 %). decode.py repeats this from key_first_page.tsv.
    Limits: it is the same writer and the same cipher. The test shows that the old key was real. It does not test the new values. "The final reading keeps" uses the hand list of the reader.
  2. The blind audit of the key. A fresh reader had the three transcriptions, the cut rule, and the key with 24 values hidden. It restored 24 of 24.
    Limits: the reader is a model of the same family as the first reader. The two can share a habit. The cut was given. So the test does not test the cut, and it does not test the figures. The reader used the order of the alphabet for 19 of the 24 values; it fixed 5 by the sense alone. The pool holds only values with 3 places or more: the weak values of the key are not in it. The session of the reader held a one-line index of the notes of the project (titles only, no key value).
  3. The order of the alphabet, all values. The 27 words of table 1 stand in the order of the alphabet by their numbers. So do the 24 words of table 2. So do both as one list of 51. 0 of 20,000 shuffles are as good.
    Limits: the reader of f. 85r knew that table 1 was in order before it fixed any new value. Seven values of the second round and eleven of the first round were fixed "with the order in mind". So this form is not blind. With the four endings, table 2 is not in full order (rank correlation 0.598; 16 of 20,000 shuffles are as high).
  4. The order of the alphabet, strict form. Only the values that were fixed before anybody saw the order. The ten words of table 1 of the first round: in order; the chance is 1 in 3.6 million. The five words that the first page had as groups of two figures (12 ma, 29 non, 55 se, 58 si, 66 stato): put the 2 before them, and they are 212, 229, 255, 258, 266, in order; the chance is 1 in 120 (152 of 20,000 shuffles). The ten and the five as one list: 15 of 15 in order, 0 of 20,000 shuffles.
    Limits: five values in order is a weak test by itself. The strength is in the list of 15, and in the fact that nobody knew of a second table when the five were fixed.
  5. The cut rule against rival rules. Figures left over or forced into a group with no value: on f. 85r the old rule leaves 60, the new rule 2; on f. 65r 3 and 0; on f. 80r 44 and 28. The share of the letters that stand inside dictionary words of four letters or more rises from 56 to 72 % (f. 85r), from 37 to 54 % (f. 65r) and from 58 to 66 % (f. 80r). Real Italian of this writer gives 67 to 72 % by the same measure.
    Limits: two rival rules for the lone 2 score as well as ours (71.5 and 70.1 % on f. 85r). The measure cannot tell them apart. The word list is not in this repository.
  6. Shuffled keys. The four-letter score of the cipher runs of f. 85r and f. 65r is -4.148 on 510 windows. Real Italian scores -4.14 in the median. 2,000 keys with shuffled values score -5.637 in the mean and -5.072 at best. The key is 8.5 standard deviations above the mean. The true key on 2,000 shuffled orders of the groups: mean -5.253, best -5.025; the true order is 17.5 standard deviations above. For f. 80r the audit gives -4.01 with the new cut and -4.16 with the old one.
    Limits: the table of Italian four-letter groups is not in this repository. decode.py does not repeat the scores. The scores of our first page (-4.18; 8.3 and 21.2 standard deviations) belong to the old cut.
  7. The halves of f. 85r. The letters, the words of the first page and the new values that stand in lines 5 to 13 give 279 of the 289 groups of lines 14 to 25 and of f. 65r (96.5 %). The reverse gives 246 of 270 (91.1 %).
    Limits: the key was made on the whole page. "Stands in a half" means only that the group occurs there.
  8. The cipher runs that complete their clear sentences. f. 85r has 11 cipher runs between clear words, and f. 65r has 2. 13 of 13 join the clear words at both ends. 11 make a full sentence (the names stay numbers). 2 do not: "e o s t i" in line 11, and the run that ends with 169 in line 12. Three of the 11 have a doubt inside. With the key and the cut of the first page: 0 of 13 complete.
    Limits: "a full sentence" is the judgement of the reader. The clear words are one reading from tiles.
  9. Two transcription passes for each letter. f. 85r: pass A has 949 figures and signs, pass B (blind) has 920; 906 are the same (97.0 %); pass B and the settled text agree in 916 (99.6 %). f. 65r: 72 of 72. f. 80r: 1,561 of 1,595 places (97.9 %).
    Limits: pass A was not blind to the sense on any letter. Pass A of f. 85r has 29 figures too many. On f. 85r four places of the settled text differ from the blind pass, and the sense guided the choice in at least two of them.
  10. The facts, against the same writer. Two period decipherments of other cipher letters of Badalocchio, and his clear letter of 9 January, treat the same matter in the same words. f. 80r has "persevera nella sua heresia fin al quarto anno"; the decipherment of 11 December has "Volge il quarto anno" and "persevera heretico". f. 85r has the Pope's demand "che ella si ritirasse dal Navara"; the decipherment of 6 February has "me rispose che lasci Navara", and Badalocchio's answer "quello ch'altra volta gli ho detto".
    Limits: these are not decipherments of our letters. They support the sense. They do not test single groups.
  11. The facts, against printed history. Pages as the audit gives them:
    • Cardinal Gondi was stopped on his way to Rome in October 1592, under a Spanish threat: L. von Pastor, History of the Popes, vol. XXIII, pp. 67-68; H. de L'Epinois, La Ligue et les papes (1886), p. 573.
    • Spain pressed in Rome for the election of the Infanta in October 1592: L'Epinois, p. 574. Philip II proposed his daughter as Queen of France in 1593: Pastor, p. 71.
    • The four ways that were weighed in Rome in October 1592 (Navarre converted, another Bourbon prince, the Infanta, a prince of Lorraine): L'Epinois, p. 574.
    • The Pope was in France with the legate Cardinal Alessandrino in 1571: Pastor, p. 22.
    • The Pope sets Navarre beside the Queen of England at the end of January 1593: Pastor, p. 68.
    • Henri IV was king from 2 August 1589. January 1593 is in his fourth year.
    Limits: no fact of the three readings is against the books. But three facts stand only on the writer's own clear letter: Milan ill content, the King of Spain near death, and the coming of Cardinal Sforza. No book that we read names a mission of Sforza in these weeks.
  12. What was not found. Capilupi's proposal (f. 85r line 25) is not in the decipherments and not in the books. The prophecy of Cardano (f. 80r line 10) is not identified; the reading of the words is sure.

  • How f. 85r stands to f. 67r. G. Cassiani cites the clear letter of 5 December 1592 on f. 67r, for its news of Tarugi and of the archbishopric of Avignon. f. 85r bears the same date. By our inventory they are two letters of the same day: f. 67 is one leaf, clear, about 14 lines, with its own date line and its own address on the back; ff. 85-86 are a separate letter, with its own date line, its signature, and its address on f. 86v. The news of Tarugi is on f. 67r. The cipher is on f. 85r only. Both letters name Cardinal Alessandrino: by Cassiani (2014, p. 395, note 44), f. 67r says that Tarugi was in France with him; on f. 85r the Pope says the same of himself. The inventory is one reading from small images.

They are in the hand of a clerk. We found them after our first reading. The readings were made without them.
