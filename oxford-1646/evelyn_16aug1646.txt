  "... yet I thinke it not necessary to write to Marquis Hertford nor Earl of Southamton, for having more care of
  themselves than of my honnor, by yielding up my seales and sword to the rebels."

Basis: 107 am, 148 el, 319 re, 383 up, 122 by, 365 then come from the Titus sister key (103 am, 144 el, 313 re, 383 up,
118 by, 364 then); 258 more and 133 care, 431 yield from the sense and the order of the list (you 429, your 430, yield
431); the letters a 46, l 75, e 70, s 57-59, t 43, h 84 are all confirmed in f.10 (Marston, Bartholomew, terms,
Southward, Headington).
Doubtful: 258 more (could be "much" = Titus 257), 133 care (Titus 129 not seen), "there" in the print (a clear word
of the King or a gloss; we read 365 = then).
Sense and date agree with the fact: Oxford gave up the Great Seal and the other seals and the sword of state in
June 1646 (not checked today in a source; from general knowledge).
Novelty: Bray (1857) left this part without decipherment; Aymeloglu (16 Sept 2026) and cipher-lab (2 Oct 2026) took
only the glossed words from it. No reading of the tail found (grep of their notes, 4 Oct 2026).
