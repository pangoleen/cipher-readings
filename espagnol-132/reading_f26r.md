# Philip II to Juan de Vargas Mexía, March 1578 (first page only) — BnF Espagnol 132, f.26r

Gallica `btv1b10032556x`, canvas 28 (right page). Cipher: "Vargas Mexia's Cipher 2" (key broken and published by S. Tomokiyo, 2020).
BnF record: art. 12 of "Sept lettres … Madrid, 8 et 16 mars, et S. Lorenzo, 16 et 17 mars 1578"; art. 13 (f.28) is its duplicate.

**Limits.** Only f.26r is online. The Gallica scan goes from f.26r (canvas 28) straight to the address leaf before f.32
(canvas 29, docket "16 de março de 1578"). Folios 26v to 31r are not in the scan: the rest of this letter, its date and signature,
its duplicate (f.28) and art. 14 (f.30). So the text below is the first 27 lines and it stops in the middle of a sentence.
The date is not on the page; the docket on the next scanned leaf says 16 March 1578 (not proved to belong to this letter).

**Status.** First reading with the known key, as far as I can find. Cabinet Noir names this page ("lettre E, f.26r, non comptée,
étendue douteuse") and publishes no reading of it. No decipherment is on the page. Simancas minute not seen.

**Passes.** Two blind passes by agents that had only the sign names and the line tiles (A and B), 27 lines, 545 and 537 groups;
agreement 436/545 = 80.0 % of groups (most differences are the place of a dot or the lone "y" sign). Decoded from pass A, checked
against pass B. Wrong-key control: see NOTES.md.

## Spanish (decoded; [ ] = restored; (?) = doubtful)

Juan de Vargas Mexia [in clear]. He visto lo que me escrivis cerca de la yda de Stucley a Inglaterra con aquell[a] nave y gente que
Su Santidad le dio, y ha sido muy acertado avisarme dello [y de] su discurr[ir](?) sobr'ello(?). Y aunque hasta agora yo no he tenido
ni tengo parte en la nave y gente que Su Santidad le dio, visto de la manera que la Reyna de Inglaterra procede, y las ayudas que ha
dado y ofrecido(?) al de Oranges y a los Estados, no seria fuera de proposito desassossegarla por esta via, para que con tener que
hazer en su casa dexasse la agena. Y assi, haviendome avisado el dicho Stucley de [su] llegada a Palamos con e[l] navio y gente,
pidiendome que yo le mandasse socorrer con algun dinero para pagar alguna infanteria y cavalleria que pensava levantar en Yrlanda,
le he mandado proveer de veynte mill escudos en oro, con persona propria, secretamente, para este efeto; de lo qual os he querido
advertir para que lo tengays entendido, solamente para vos mismo, y assi converna tener dello mucho secreto.

Pareceme muy bien lo que advertis, que se haga algun regalo al secretario Villeroy por las causas que dezis; y […]

## English

Juan de Vargas Mexía. I have seen what you write to me about the voyage of Stukeley to England with that ship and the men that
His Holiness gave him. You did very well to tell me of it and of your (?) thoughts on it. Up to now I have had no part, and I have
none, in the ship and men that His Holiness gave him. But I see how the Queen of England behaves, and the help that she has given
and offered (?) to the Prince of Orange and to the States. So it would not be out of place to unsettle her by this road: with work
to do in her own house she would leave the house of others alone. The said Stukeley has told me of his arrival at Palamós with the
ship and men, and he has asked me for some money to pay some foot and horse that he means to raise in Ireland. I have therefore
ordered twenty thousand escudos in gold to be provided to him, by a man of my own, in secret, for this purpose. I wished to tell
you of this, for yourself alone; it must be kept very secret.

I think it very good, as you advise, to make some present to the secretary Villeroy, for the reasons that you give; and […]

## Notes

- "desassossegarla": the cipher has `de-sa-ss-o | ss-e-ga-r-la` over the line break (pass A; pass B the same letters).
- "Stucley": spelled `s-[null]-tu-c-l-e-y` in L2 and `e-s-tu-c-[..]-y` in L13.
- "veynte mill escudos en oro": `ue-y-n-te mi-ll e-s-cu-do-s e-n o-ro` (both passes).
- "Villeroy": `ui-ll-e-ro-y`, Nicolas de Neufville, seigneur de Villeroy, French secretary of state.
- The fact that Stukeley put in at Palamós in February-March 1578 with the Pope's ship is known (Z. N. Brooke, EHR 1913;
  Tazón, 2003). I did not find this letter or the gift of 20,000 escudos in print in the time I had; I did not read the
  Calendar of State Papers, Simancas, for March 1578 page by page. Treat the novelty as "not found", not as "unpublished".
