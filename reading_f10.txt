--- ENGLISH (modern spelling; the sense where the reading holds) ---

May it please your Majesty. Since my letter of the fifth of May I have sent four copies (?) with this one.
[Fairfax?] has finished his bridge at Marston [...] on [Headington] hill. He is making several lines from that
hill: one towards Dover Pier (?), the other towards St Bartholomew's Hospital. And he [sent] to have Oxford
rendered to him for the use of the Parliament, and said that the Governor may have honourable terms for himself
and all within the garrison if he accepts in good time. The direction was that they asked a safe conduct for
Sir John Monson and Mr [Warwick] to come to [confer] with Fairfax, which he [gave] the next day. They were told,
as from the Governor (?), to ask leave to send a messenger to your Majesty. They pressed Fairfax for this, but he
absolutely refuses it. The reason why these gentlemen were sent to treat with Fairfax was that we might learn
[...] what condition [...]; for since your departure we have received not a word from your Majesty, nor news of
you, except what has come out of the enemy's quarters. Nothing comes from London, because we are very closely
shut in. We hear, and the print (?) of Lothian's letter to the Committee of Both Kingdoms shows the same, that
your Majesty is in a very sad condition. But I believe [...] that the letter was written [...] with the Scots
until they gather a considerable body together [...], which might give their friends at London confidence to
stay firm to them. [...] comfort and encourage your faithful subjects here to hold out. For those whom your
Majesty thought (?) most averse are now extremely earnest for a treaty (?), in the hope to save their interest (?).
We pay and feed (?) here nearly four thousand foot. This is one thousand [less?] than double the number (?) that
we provided for, so our [stores] will fall short. Besides, very many of your Majesty's poor (?) [...] are here.
It is believed that we shall not have a treaty for more than two days from now. [If] the Scots should face
southward with a considerable body, it might perhaps free us of our ill neighbours. We hear that they have
[settled] themselves so [...] that we believe they do not intend to stir [until they have] six thousand foot
from London. But the differences there between the Presbyterians and the Independents, I believe, have kept
those men [away] so far. From the West I hear that the Prince (?) is for certain in Jersey (?), where he resolved
to wait for your Majesty's directions. [Yesterday?] Prince Rupert, skirmishing with some of Fairfax's horse,
received [a shot in] his right arm (?). But thanks be to God there is no [danger] in it, as it is a flesh wound (?);
only it makes him feverish (?). We hear daily from Fairfax's quarters such sad news [...]. They first planted (?)
on Sunday a piece [of cannon ...] of Headington hill and have made several shots into Oxford, but have done no
great hurt. God preserve [you] from your [...], which is the earnest prayer of your sacred Majesty's most
obedient servant, [Nicholas] (?). 13 May 1646.
