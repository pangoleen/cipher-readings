# The Duke of Nevers to Tommaso Bozio, 1 May 1593: reading of the two cipher passages

Source: Paris, BnF, ms. français 4700, no. 38, f. 72r (Gallica `btv1b90582908`, view 84). Key: [key.tsv](key.tsv),
rebuilt from the text. Transcription: [transcription.txt](transcription.txt). State: 11 October 2026; corrected on
14 October 2026 after a review panel (passage 1: the subject; passage 2: "would come").

The work was done by a model. No palaeographer has checked it.

Marks: a raised dot = the null 0 of the cipher. [?] = a mark that we cannot read. (?) = doubtful. Letters in square
brackets in the normalised text = letters that the writer left out. In the English, words in square brackets = a
place that we cannot translate with confidence. u and v are one letter in the cipher.

## Passage 1

It follows the clear words "il salutifero rimedio".

The letters as the cipher gives them, with our word division:

> se c pa·[?]pa(?)· uolese mandar a neu·ers un suo· confidente io gli faria saper tu·to che desideraria

Normalised Italian:

> se c pa[?]pa(?) volesse mandar a Nevers un suo confidente, io gli faria saper tutto che desideraria.

English:

> if [not resolved; perhaps the Pope] would send to Nevers a confidant of his, I would let him know all that he would wish.

**The first words are not sure.** Read this before you quote the passage.

- The third group is 84 on the page. Its value is c. So the page has "se c pa...". It does not have "se 'l papa".
- "se 'l papa" (if the Pope) is the probable sense. It needs 89 (l) in the place of 84. The writer made other
  slips in this draft. But we do not correct the page: we give the letters as they stand.
- After "pa" and a null stands a mark that we cannot read. Then "p", a figure that is a 3 or a 2, and a null.
  With 3 = a the letters are "pa·[?]pa·", and "papa" is the probable reading. The value 3 = a has no other
  witness. So "papa" rests on a mark that we cannot read and on a value with one witness: the English does not
  make the Pope the subject.
- The clear text before the passage speaks of "sua Santità". This fits "the Pope" as the subject of "volesse".

## Passage 2

It follows the clear words "senza spesa alcuna".

The letters as the cipher gives them, with our word division:

> di qel tale che u·enise peche sarebe spesato da mi n tuto· e tenu·tolo mo·lt secreto

Normalised Italian:

> di quel tale che venisse, pe[r]ché sarebbe spesato da me in tutto, e tenutolo molt[o] secreto.

English:

> for the man who would come, because I would pay all his costs, and he would be kept very secret.

- "qel" for "quel": the value q for 99 rests on this one word.
- "peche" for "perché": the writer left the r out. The p of this word is a correction above the line, and we read
  it in part from the sense.
- "da mi n tuto": one i serves for "mi" and "in".
- "molt secreto": the o of "molto" is not written.

## The passages in their place

Clear text in plain type (our reading from a small image, one pass; see [cleartext_f72r.txt](cleartext_f72r.txt)).
Cipher in **bold**, in the normalised form.

> ... Io giudicare(?) come et necessario et primo(?) il salutifero rimedio **se c pa[?]pa(?) volesse mandar a Nevers
> un suo confidente, io gli faria saper tutto che desideraria.** Il che si potra fare facilmente, essendovi un
> Colegio de Gesuiti del quale il Rettore è Italiano et predicatore(?) ... Et una Comunità de frati dominicani
> francesi tutti. la Cità è grande assai et si faria il tutto senza spesa alcuna **di quel tale che venisse,
> pe[r]ché sarebbe spesato da me in tutto, e tenutolo molt[o] secreto.** Pero degno(?) Molto Reverendo Padre ...

English:

> ... I judge(?) as necessary and first(?) the salutary remedy: **if [not resolved; perhaps the Pope] would send to
> Nevers a confidant of his, I would let him know all that he would wish.** This can be done easily, because there is a
> college of Jesuits whose rector is an Italian and a preacher(?) ... and a community of Dominican friars, all
> French. The town is rather large, and all would be done without any cost **for the man who would come, because I
> would pay all his costs, and he would be kept very secret.** ...

In short: Nevers asks for a man of trust from Rome, who would come to the town of Nevers. He offers to pay for
him and to keep him secret. If "papa" is right, the man is to come from the Pope.

## Forms of the writer

- Single consonants: "uolese", "uenise", "sarebe", "tuto". The cipher has no sign for a doubled letter.
- "faria", "desideraria": the conditional in -ia.
- "spesato": paid for, with his costs borne.

## What is not read

- The mark in passage 1 (see above).
- The first draft that the writer struck out in the second line of passage 2 (about 15 signs).
- The verso of the leaf. We did not see it.
