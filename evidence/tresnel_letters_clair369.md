# Tresnel's letters in Clairambault 369 — check for cipher (4 October 2026)

Question: do the letters of the marquis de Tresnel in Clair 369 (ff. 68, 70, 252, 321, 323, "etc." in the BnF notice)
carry the cipher of fr. 18009 f. 156?

Answer: no. All five letters are in clear from the first word to the signature. There is nothing to decipher.

Method: one reduced image (1500 px wide) of each opening, read by me (one pass). Images in `img/clair369/`.

"etc." in the notice: I looked at every opening from f. 324 to the end of the volume (f. 339, canvases 336-359).
There is no other letter of Tresnel and no cipher there. The folios 1-67, 71-251 and 253-311 were not scanned by me.
S. Tomokiyo's survey of the volume names cipher only at ff. 11, 13 (Castille), 109 (Baugy), 229, 294 (Du Maurier)
and 316 (Cocquet).

What the clear letters give for the Cocquet letter (words read on the previews, one pass):
- f. 68: Tresnel waits "avec impatience … le retour de Coquet". So Coquet was away at court in October 1616.
- f. 321: "… que m'a apport[é] Coquet, lequel m'aiant expos[é] les commandemens que me faict V. M. …". So Coquet came
  back to Rome just before 13 November, the date of his own letter (Clair 369 f. 316).
- f. 323 starts "Coquet m'aiant fait savoir les bons offices qu'il vous a pleu me departir …".

### Du Nozet, 13 March 1617 (Clair 372 f. 71r): a second, different cipher — not read

Transcription, one pass, from `img/clair372/f71_cipher.jpg` (digits and letters as written; · = dot on the page):

"Il me reste Monseigneur a vous dire qu'un mien amy me mande que **23433 · iiμh53x ·** avoit tout a fait refusé
de **33366ih ·** la **8i5773 ·** de ce qu'il scait touchant l'affaire de **20i** et de **6ix 757m26c** ; je m'estonne
come il l'ose faire puis que luy mesme **8532δ96226** et qui m'estonne le plus est qu'on m'escrit que
**23433 85x(+)π98 ·** faict le mesme reffus. J'ay ceste consolation en moy mesme d'avoir satisfaict a ma conscience …"

- About 57 signs in 9 groups: figures 0-9 mixed with i, h, x, m, c, μ, δ, π. The group 23433 comes twice.
- Test with the Tresnel key: the key has no sign i, h (plain), 0, 7, 9 in its alphabet, and its numbers are names
  and words. "23 4 33" would be "M. de R… / la royne / le grand maistre de Malthe". No sense. The key does not apply.
- No decipherment on the page. Not in Tomokiyo's article (he names only f. 169 for Clair 372). Not attacked: it is
  too short for a solution that a control can prove.

## fr. 18009 (Rome, 1614): what stands near the key leaf

Reduced images (1000 px) of canvases 186-202 = ff. 161-176 (letters of 14-29 May 1614), one pass. The sweep was
stopped there. Canvases 203-396 (ff. 177-358, June-December 1614) were NOT looked at.

- No letter in the Tresnel cipher on these leaves. Tresnel was still on his way: Brèves writes from Avignon on
  29 May 1614 (f. 174) "J'ay veu a Marseille Mons. le Marquis de Tresnel …". His own dispatches from Rome begin
  later in the volume. They are the place to look for this cipher with a period decipherment.
- f. 168r-v (canvases 193-194), Du Nozet to [Puisieux], Rome, 25 May 1614: three short cipher groups WITH a period
  decipherment above the line. Full-size image of f. 168r: `img/fr18009/c193.jpg`, crop `f168_cipher.jpg`. I read:
  - a sign like a capital gamma, gloss "cardinal borghese";
  - `2 h 6(?) 9 7 w 3 0 g 0(?) x` (11 signs), gloss "Aldobrandin" (11 letters);
  - `28` (underlined), gloss "opinion".
  This is Du Nozet's own cipher: figures and letters for letters, numbers for words. It looks like the cipher of
  his letter of 13 March 1617 (Clair 372 f. 71r), but the 1614 values do not read the 1617 groups at once
  (for example 7 = b, 3 = a would give "…bba" for `8i5773`). Either the table changed or the figures of 1617 must be
  cut in pairs. More glossed Du Nozet letters of 1614 are needed (fr. 18009 has several; f. 168v has two more
  groups). This is a lead for a separate task. Not done here.
