# Vincenzo Badalocchio to the Duke of Nevers, Rome, 9(?) January 1593: the cipher sheet read for about four fifths, with gaps

A sheet of 18 lines, all in figures, from the agent of the Duke of Nevers in Rome. It is in the cipher of the
Duke's minute of 1 May 1593 ([nevers-1593](../nevers-1593/)). This folder gives a partial reading of it.

**Status: 11 October 2026.** The work was done by a model. No palaeographer has checked it. **The reading is
partial.** Corrections are welcome: please open an issue.

## What this is

- The leaf is a bifolium, ff. 79-80. F. 79r-v is a clear letter of Vincenzo Badalocchio to the Duke of Nevers.
  F. 80r is the cipher sheet. F. 80v is the address.
- The clear letter is signed "Vincenzio Badalocchio". Its date line is "In Roma: il dì ix(?) di Gennaro: MDXCIII".
- **The day is read with doubt.** We read "ix" (9). "x" alone (10) is less probable. The year and the month are
  clear. Badalocchio's other letters of this winter are dated on Saturdays, and 9 January 1593 is a Saturday.
  The detail of the page is in `images/f79v_date.jpg`.
- The cipher sheet has 18 lines: 1,549 figures, 6 dotted strokes, 20 signs, two numbers in a drawn frame, and
  one blot that we cannot read. It has no clear word and no decipherment.
- Vincenzo Badalocchio was the agent in Rome of Ludovico Gonzaga, Duke of Nevers. He died on 19 February 1593
  (G. Cassiani).
- By our count the bifolium is no. 42 of the volume. The catalogue gives the numbers 39 to 46 to the letters of
  Badalocchio, with the note "En italien. Chiffre.", and it gives no folio for each number. So "no. 42" is
  inferred.
- The cipher is the cipher of the Duke's minute to Tommaso Bozio of 1 May 1593. That minute, its key and the
  history of the two ciphers of Badalocchio are in the folder [nevers-1593](../nevers-1593/). This folder does
  not repeat them.

## The clear letter, in a few lines

The clear letter gives the news of Rome and of Italy. Our transcription is in
[evidence/clear_letter_f79.md](evidence/clear_letter_f79.md). It is a reading by a model, from one pass.

- The Pope is well again.
- Messina is in unrest, and its people do not trust the viceroy of Sicily. At Naples the viceroy put some lords
  in prison, and the nobles are "mezza solevata".
- At Milan the Constable of Castile made the Senate stand, with bare heads. The Senate and the nobles are "assai
  alterati, e male sodisfatti".
- The King of Spain is ill: "poco si spera della vita sua".
- News of Cardinal Sforza, of the quarrel of the Grand Duke of Tuscany with his brother, and of offices at the
  court of Rome.
- Mons. Malvasia goes to France and has an order to treat with the Duke. The opinion in Rome is that Navarre "sia
  per farsi cattolico".

The cipher sheet speaks of the same things: Milan, Naples and Sicily, the death of a prince, and the heresy of
Navarre.

## What is new

**This is the first reading known to us; the key is the one rebuilt from the Duke's minute of 1 May 1593 (folder
nevers-1593), extended from this sheet; no key sheet of this cipher is known.**

- **It is a partial reading.** About four fifths of the text stand in words that we translate. The rest is given
  as gaps.
- "Known to us" has limits. The searches are listed under "Earlier work", with what we did not open.
- The letters of the key are those of the minute, unchanged. This sheet adds the vowel 5, the null 2, six signs,
  eight groups of two figures (one of them has no value yet), and a table of short words with three figures.
- No name is read. The four name groups have no value.

## The cipher

The cipher text is a row of figures with no gaps. The cut rule goes from left to right:

- **A group that starts with 1 has three figures.** It is a word or a syllable from a table in the order of the
  alphabet: 141 che, 142 chi, 143 ci ... 197 lui. One group is the exception: 12 (ma) has two figures.
- **A figure 4, 8 or 9 takes the next figure.** The pair is one letter. These are the letters of the minute:
  43 c, 44 u, 45 a, 46 e, 47 i, 48 e, 49 o; 83 b, 84 c, 85 d, 86 f, 87 g, 88 h, 89 l; 93 m, 94 n, 95 p, 96 r,
  97 s, 98 t, 99 q. Two pairs are syllables: 91 ente, 92 per.
- **A figure 3, 5, 6 or 7 stands alone.** It is a vowel: a, i, o, u. Two pairs start with 5: 55 (se) and 58 (si).
  The pair 66 is "stato"; it stands two times.
- **The figures 2 and 0 stand alone and are nulls.** Five signs are nulls too. A null often stands between the
  two halves of a doubled sound: es·se-guirsi, sareb··bono, ef··ficaci.
- A sign like a triangle is the letter z.
- **A name is a figure 1 with a dot, and two more figures.** It comes from another table. No name is read.
- A number in clear stands in a drawn frame.
- u and v are one letter.

The cut is not free of doubt in two cases:

- "5 8" can be the group 58 (si), or the letter i and the head of a pair 8x. In four places we read i + 8x:
  Milano (two times), "cosi bel-", Sicilia.
- "2 9" and "3 9" can be the groups 29 and 39, or a null / the letter a and the head of a pair 9x. In two places
  we read the groups 29 and 39. We learned this cut from the held-out passage of f. 83r.

## The reading

The full reading, line by line, with the gaps left as gaps: [reading.md](reading.md).

### What Badalocchio tells the Duke, in five sentences

1. The troubles of the Spanish lands in Italy make an opening: the State of Milan is very ill content, and Navarre
   with two other powers (the names are not read) could hope to raise it and to take it with ease.
2. The death of a prince (name not read; the clear letter says that the King of Spain is near death) would add to
   this, but the heresy of Navarre and of another power makes such enterprises hard or impossible to carry out.
3. Navarre is not as prudent as his own good asks, because he still perseveres stubbornly in his heresy: if he had
   left it on the first day of his reign, he would now be at peace in France, with the hope of the monarchy that
   Cardano foretells, of which Badalocchio wrote in his letters of December, number 47.
4. The Duke is on the spot and has authority, so he must make the king a good and vigorous remonstrance, with
   reasons beyond those of the soul: a rising in Milan with French support, and Naples and Sicily would not(?)
   stay in line.
5. So fine an occasion will never come again.

Sentences 1, 2 and 4 hold gaps. Their general sense is inferred from the words around the gaps and from the
clear letter. Sentence 3 holds two gaps too: see doubt 13.

## The controls

Details: [evidence/worklog.md](evidence/worklog.md), [evidence/blind_audit.md](evidence/blind_audit.md),
[evidence/heldout_f83r.md](evidence/heldout_f83r.md).

1. **Two transcription passes, and a third reading.** Pass A was not blind to the key. Pass B was blind: a
   separate agent read the tiles only. The passes have the same sign in 1,561 of 1,595 places (97.9 %). A third
   reading then settled the weak lines. It changed some figures by the sense, not by the image: see doubt 5.
2. **A blind audit of the key.** A fresh reader had the cut text, the key with ten values hidden, and the decode
   with gaps. **It restored 10 of 10** (g, b, m, t, i, di, che, la, si, ma).
3. **The connected sense.** 617 of the 792 groups that are not nulls (78 %) stand in words that we translate.
4. **The two halves.** The key of the minute and the values that lines 1-9 fix give a value to 421 of the 443
   groups of lines 10-18 (95.0 %). The reverse gives 445 of 476 (93.5 %).
5. **The four-letter score.** The full key scores -4.18. Real Italian scores -4.14 in the median, and -4.61 at
   worst in 300 samples.
6. **Shuffled keys.** 3,000 keys with shuffled values score -5.96 in the mean and -5.29 at best. Our key is 8.3
   standard deviations above the mean.
7. **Shuffled orders.** The true key on 3,000 shuffled orders of the groups scores -5.42 in the mean and -5.21 at
   best. The true order is 21.2 standard deviations above the mean.
8. **The order of the word table.** The 17 sure words of the table stand in the order of the alphabet, by their
   numbers: 17 of 17.
9. **A held-out passage.** A short cipher passage stands in Badalocchio's clear letter of 6 February 1593
   (f. 83r). It had no part in the making of the key. The key, unchanged, gives a value to 32 of its 33 groups.
   "il [..] di Pavia" and "co·se di Navara" read: 18 of the 29 groups that are not nulls. Both names stand in the
   clear text of that letter.
10. **The content.** The sheet speaks of the things that the clear letter of the same leaf reports: see below.

The limits of each control:

- **The blind audit used a reader of the same model family as the first reader.** The two can share a wrong
  habit. **The auditor was given the cut.** So the audit does not test the cut rule, and it does not test the
  figures. It shows that the hidden values follow from the other values and from the Italian. The ten hidden
  groups all stand 8 times or more.
- **The held-out passage is short, and it is not fully blind.** It has 33 groups. By the first cut, "ENTE m" (two
  places) and "s c i u e" did not read. The passage then taught two values of the cut: 29 and 39 are groups. We
  used them on this sheet. The figures of the passage have one pass.
- **The halves:** we made the key on the whole sheet. "Fixed in a half" means only that a clear word of that
  half fixes the value. Lines 1-9 cannot supply z, et, far, io, lui, dove and non. Lines 10-18 cannot supply per,
  ci, come, egli, fa, in, ben and fatto.
- **The order of the word table:** ten of the 17 words were fixed from the sense before we saw the order (che,
  chi, ci, di, et, fa, far, il, la, le). The chance that ten values fall in order is 1 in 3.6 million. Seven were
  fixed with the order in mind (come, da, egli, giorno, in, io, lui). They are not independent.
- **The count of the connected sense** is a count by hand, by the reader. Nobody else made it.
- **The scores** need a table of Italian four-letter groups that is not in this repository. `decode.py` repeats
  the counts, the halves, the order of the table and the held-out passage. It does not repeat the scores.
- "Groups with a value" (97.3 %) proves little. A shuffled key gives a value to the same groups.

- The addressee is with the king and has standing: "Ella che si trova presente et con autorità ... deve far una
  buona et gagliarda rimostranza". G. Cassiani describes this work: Badalocchio was the Duke's agent for the
  reconciliation of Henri IV.
- Henri IV was king from August 1589. January 1593 is in his fourth year. This fits "il primo giorno del suo
  regno", and "il quarto" if "year" is meant.

What does not fit, or is not checked:

- "la monarchia che predice il Cardano": the words are sure. We did not find the prediction of Girolamo Cardano
  that is meant. G. Cassiani (2016) shows that prophecies of a new monarchy of Henri IV were current in Rome in
  1592.
- No word "Papa", "impresa" (singular) or "conversione" stands on the sheet. "imprese" (plural) does.
- The names are not read. "The King of Spain" for 112 and "England" for 117 are guesses.

## Doubts

1. **The reading is partial.** Three stretches and all names have no reading. A gap is left as a gap.
2. **The day of the date** is "ix(?)". "x" (10) is possible.
3. **The number of the piece** (no. 42) is inferred by a count of the bifolia.
4. **The cut** is not free of doubt in two cases: "5 8", and "2 9" or "3 9". We settled six places by the sense.
5. **Some figures were settled by the sense, not by the image.** Line 7: 148, where 145 is possible. Line 12: "4 6"
   after the frame, where a blot is joined to the 6; and a 6 where pass B saw a sign. Line 17: a 5 where pass B
   read "(7)". Line 18: "83 4 6" and "8 4 3", both blots. One blot of line 18 is not settled ("5 ? 6").
6. **The scan** is from black-and-white film. Blots hide figures in lines 12, 17 and 18.
7. **Four values are only probable:** 29 (non), 135 (ben), 154 (dove), 163 (fatto). Each has one place on this
   sheet.
8. **92 = per** fits "persevera". It does not fit "per si mostra" and "per i tr". Five of its seven places do not
   make a clear word.
9. **Fourteen groups have no value:** 132, 133, 138, 145, 166, 167, 181, 194, 198, 199, 100, 41, 80 and 39. The
   order of the alphabet allows guesses for four of them (132 al.., 138 bene, 166 fin, 167 fosse). These guesses
   are not in the key.
10. **The names are not read.** 117 stands three times, 112, 138 and 178 once. 178 is in a damaged place.
11. **The letter n after a null** stands in five places and makes no word: "spera·n", "uo·n", "·n et", "tr·n",
    "un ·n".
12. **Single words:** "sole·uo·n" (sollevatione?) in line 2; "ra·nb··be" in line 7, which we read as
    "[diside]ra[r]ebbe": the sheet has n where the sense asks for r; "rella" (quella?) and "dovere(?)" in line 10;
    "non(?)" in line 16; "occasi[on]e" in line 18, where one figure is a blot. Line 2 has "spera·n di far", where
    "sperando" is expected.
13. **Sentence 3 of the summary.** The words that we read are "si mostra prudente come disidera[r]ebbe": "shows
    himself prudent as one would wish". The sentence holds two gaps ("c[..]sse", and the group 138). The summary
    takes it as a reproach, because of the words that follow. This is our view of the sense.
14. **The two numbers in a frame.** Line 11: xij (12) or xxij (22). Line 12: 47, with a blotted 4.
15. **The sign &** in line 3 has no value.
16. **The count of line 3** in the table of counts does not agree with the text (see "The state").
17. **The facts of standard history** in the work log are from memory: the Constable of Castile at Milan, the
    viceroy of Sicily, the date of the abjuration. We did not check them against a book.
18. **The clear letter** is one pass from tiles. Words with (?) are not sure.
19. **Pass A was not blind to the key.** The two passes differ in 34 places.
20. **We did not see views 96 to 100** of the volume (ff. 83v-88). The passage of f. 83r is the only other
    cipher text of Badalocchio that we saw in ff. 73-83.

  Badalocchio is his no. 56. A note on the back of that key says that it was replaced in September 1592. The
  folder [nevers-1593](../nevers-1593/) gives the note and the reasons why the cipher of 1593 is another one.
  His pages, as far as we had them saved, do not name fr. 4700. His blog feed had nothing on this sheet (the 25
  newest posts, up to 11 October 2026).
- `NoAutopilot/cipher-lab`: no work on fr. 4700.
