# The audit of 12 October 2026: the name group .176, the short group .38, and the group 167

This file is the public copy of an audit of three changes to the reading of Badalocchio's sheet of 9(?) January
1593 (BnF fr. 4700, f. 80r). A second reader proposed the three changes on 12 October 2026. A separate agent
then made this audit. It did not make the reading. Its task was to test the changes. The second reader and the
auditor are models. No palaeographer has checked this audit.

The second reader's report and the working images are not in this repository. This copy leaves out the names of
working files. It changes no verdict and no source.

Marks. **SEEN** = the auditor read it on the day of the audit, on an image or in a book text. **INFERRED** = a
conclusion of the auditor.

This audit overrules part 5.3 of the first audit ([audit.md](audit.md)) for the groups .176 and .38.

## Verdicts in short

| Change | Verdict | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. .176 = Lesdiguières ("la Dighiera"), not the Queen of England | **ACCEPT. Grade: probable.** Name the Queen as the rival | The group has an army "much feared", and with it Navarre can take Milan "very easily". England had no army near Italy. Five dispatches from Rome of the same four weeks name Lesdiguières for this danger, and one names "his heresies". "La Dighiera", feminine, is a real form of the 1590s |
| 2. ".38" is 138 (or .138) and its value is "cattolici" | **REJECT as a value of the key. Print "cattolici" only as a guess for the sense of the place.** The form of the group stays open | The plain group 138 in line 7 does not read with "cattolico". The change needs a lost stroke or a dot by a slip. The image shows one dotted stroke and no second stroke |
| 3. 167 = "fosse" | **ACCEPT. Grade: probable** | "fosse" + r + o gives the verb that the sentence needs. No other word between "fin" and "Francia" makes a word with "ro". A period decipherment of another letter of Badalocchio has "fossero", not "fussero". One place only |

No name is read from the cipher. All three results are guesses with grades.

### A.2 The second reader's quotations, checked

**Paruta, 12 December 1592** (Bartoli pp. 102-105). SEEN:

- p. 102, the Pope: he "prevedeva questi moti di Laodighiera dover apportare disturbo grande alla Italia".
- p. 103, the Pope: "oggidì in Italia ne' sudditi di ogni stato vi sono de' mali umori".
- p. 104, Paruta's own judgement: the plan of a league is "tutta invenzione degli Spagnuoli". The cause is "il
  pericolo che soprasta al Marchesato di Saluzzo ed al Ducato di Milano dai progressi di Laodi[g]hiera".
- p. 104, the Pope: he "non poteva credere, che questo Laodighiera avesse lungamente a sostentarsi da sè stesso".
- p. 104, Paruta: "quanto a Laodighiera, non era il suo esercito novo".

**Is the quotation fair?** Yes, with three notes.

1. The words on Saluzzo and Milan are Paruta's reading of the Spanish motive. They are not the Pope's words. The
   second reader's table says so. His summary line ("from the Pope's mouth") says a little too much.
2. The second reader wrote "dalli progressi". The printing of 1871 has "dai progressi". This changes nothing.
3. The second reader left out the two lines that speak against a great fear: the Pope doubts that Lesdiguières
   can last alone, and Paruta says that the army is not new. A fair summary must have them.

**Galluzzi, book V** (vol. 3, pp. 79-80). SEEN: "i progressi di Lesdiguieres in Piemonte e il timore di una segreta
intelligenza di esso con la Republica e con il G. Duca tenevano gli Spagnoli in apprensione per lo Stato di
Milano". The Grand Duke paid him in secret. "Il Papa proponeva ai Principi Italiani una Lega difensiva per
discacciare Lesdiguieres dal Piemonte". The quotation is fair. Note: Galluzzi says that the Spaniards feared for
Milan. He does not say that Rome did.

**Pastor XXIII p. 68.** SEEN. The Pope speaks to the Venetian ambassador "at the end of January, 1593". He says
that Navarre is a heretic and will stay one, and that he wants to do as the Queen of England did. This is a
comparison of two heretic rulers. It says nothing of an army, of Italy or of Milan. Its date is after the sheet.

### A.3 More witnesses

**For Lesdiguières.** All SEEN.

1. Paruta, 19 December 1592 (Bartoli p. 108): the Pope holds to the League, "ora massimamente che le cose del re
   di Navarra vanno prosperando, e che si può temere che i moti di Laodichiera gli vadino facendo strada a cose
   maggiori". The same dispatch (p. 106): the league "si farà contra eretici, contra Turchi".
2. Paruta, 26 December 1592 (p. 110): "Questi moti di Laodichiera occupano ora tutti i ragionamenti de' principali
   della corte, e si comincia a stimarli da dovero". Those who told the Pope not to hear Navarre "si sono tutti
   smarriti".
3. Paruta, 2 January 1593 (p. 113): the friends of Spain in Rome say that Venice lets heretics infect Italy; "già
   da questi è publicato che Laodighiera conduchi seco insieme con i soldati molti predicatori delle sue eresie;
   con le quali, non meno che con l'armi, abbia congiurato alla rovina dell'Italia". **This is the pair of the
   sheet: his heresy and his arms, in Rome, one week before the sheet.**
4. Paruta, 9 January 1593, the day of the sheet (the printing of 1864, pp. 15-16): the authors of the league want
   "di rimuovere il re di Navarra, ed altri che seguono il suo partito, da pensare al presente al venir in
   Italia"; the Pope hears each day "di ajuti dati a Laodighiera".
5. Dufayard pp. 157-162: Lesdiguières crossed into Piedmont in the autumn of 1592, fortified Bricherasio, won at
   Vigone, and took Cavour on 6 December 1592. "Désormais Lesdiguières était maître de tout le haut Piémont." He
   left his troops in winter quarters at Bricherasio and Cavour, with 50 companies of foot on the border, and
   reached Grenoble on 10 January 1593. So on 9 January his army stood in Piedmont. He came back in April 1593.
6. The clear letter of the same day (f. 79r; our transcription is [clear_letter_f79.md](clear_letter_f79.md)):
   Milan calls back "i cavalli, che si erano mandati per aiuto al Duca di Savoia". That is the war against
   Lesdiguières.

**Against, or limits.**

1. The Pope and Paruta both make the army smaller on 12 December (A.2, note 3).
2. The five dispatches are a choice by an editor of 1871 on one subject, the league. The auditor did not read
   the other dispatches of these weeks.
3. The names in these five dispatches, counted: Lesdiguières 11 times; England, the Queen, Germany, the Swiss,
   the Grisons and Geneva 0 times. This shows whom Rome feared for Italy. It does not show whom Badalocchio put
   in his key.

INFERRED:

- **"la heresia del Re et della [.176]" is good Italian for him**, if the entry is "Dighiera". The feminine
  article is attested from 1588 to 1603, in letters to Rome and in a Roman pen. The two words "della Dighiera"
  with no title were not found in a text of the 1590s. "la Dighiera" was found there (1588, 1593, 1594, 1598,
  1599), and "della Dighiera" in 1600.
- "della" is one usage of several. Paruta, in Rome in the same weeks, uses a fused form with no article. The
  second reader's line "the Italian form of his name takes 'della'" is too strong. Right: one common form takes
  it.
- **"se Navara, [.176] ..." and "havendo [.176] uno essercito" have no article.** The bare name is rare in the
  books. But the writer does the same with another name group: f. 85r has "il [.143]" in line 5 and "di [.143]"
  in line 15. He writes "Navara", "il Navara" and "dal Navara". So a group with and without the article is his
  habit.
- The article is a small fault for every candidate. No candidate gives all three places with no fault.

### A.5 England and the other rivals, each in the three places

The entry must (a) be a heretic, (b) take "della", (c) stand with Navarre as one who can raise Milan and take it
"facilissimamente", (d) have "uno essercito molto temuto in [a place]".

| Candidate | (a) heretic | (b) "della" | (c) Milan | (d) the army | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lesdiguières** (Dighiera) | Yes: "delle sue eresie" (Paruta, 2 January 1593); "la Dighiera heretica" (1599) | Yes, one attested form | Yes: Saluzzo and Milan in danger from his progress (Paruta; Galluzzi) | Yes: in Piedmont, winter quarters at Bricherasio and Cavour | **Fits all four** |
| The Queen of England | Yes | Yes, and best: "del Re et della Regina" | No. No talk in Rome of England against Milan was found | No. Her troops stood in Brittany and Normandy. No source calls them feared in these weeks, or near Italy | Fails (c) and (d) |
| Germany, the Protestant princes | Yes | "della Germania" can stand; "Prencipi" cannot | No source for these weeks | No army of theirs stood near Italy in January 1593 (not checked in a book) | Fails (c) and (d) |
| The Swiss, the Grisons | In part | No: "delli Svizzeri", "delli Grisoni". "della Lega Grisa" can stand | The Grisons border on Milan | No source calls them an army "molto temuto" in these weeks | Fails (b) or (d) |
| Geneva | Yes | "di Ginevra" is the usual form | No | No army | Fails |
| Savoy | No: a Catholic prince, at war with Lesdiguières | Yes | No | His army is the beaten one | Fails (a) |

What speaks for England, said fairly: the pair "Re" and "Regina" is the plainest Italian; Badalocchio's cipher
letter of 11 December 1592 has "la regina d'Inghilterra et gli protestanti" (the period decipherment:
[period_decipherments.md](period_decipherments.md)); the Pope set Navarre beside the Queen at the end of January
(Pastor). These points show that England was in his mind. They do not give England an army that can take Milan.
The clause "havendo [.176] uno essercito molto temuto in ..." is the reason that the writer gives for
"facilissimamente". A candidate must pass it.

## B. The place sign after "temuto in" (line 3)

SEEN on a crop of line 3 of the Gallica image, enlarged four times. After 185 (*in*) stands one sign, then 157
(*et*), then three nulls.

- The sign is one drawn form. It has a closed body with two rooms, a small one over a large one, like a square 8
  or a B with no stem. A hooked stroke crosses its right side and ends in a tail to the lower right, like an x.
- It looks most like an "&" of the time (E and t joined), or like "E" joined to "x".
- It is not a group of figures. It has no dot. It is not one of the six null signs of the key.
- **It cannot be read as letters of "Piemonte", "Italia", "Savoia" or "Delfinato".** There is no P with a stem,
  no I, no S, no D. The auditor does not force it.
- INFERRED: it is a sign of the key for one word, most likely a place. The older key of Badalocchio had entries
  for "Italia" and "Piamonte" (by the second reader's list of that key, which is not in this repository). The
  sense fits both. One place only. **Open.**
- The sign does not decide change 1. "In Piedmont" and "in Italy" are right for Lesdiguières. No place near
  Milan is right for England.

- **The second reader's judgement on line 7 is too kind.** "Only with effort" should be "it does not read". The
  auditor tried the words before and after, and found no reading of line 7 with "cattolico".
- A fair limit: line 7 is damaged before this place (three lone 2 and "c s se" are not read). So the break is
  not a clean proof against the word. But it is the only test place, and it gives no support.
- No word of the window reads in both places.
- The change also needs one of two repairs of the sheet: a lost stroke (then the group is the **name** .138, and
  the plain 138 of line 7 is another table) or a dot by a slip (then it is the **word** 138, and line 7 must
  read). The first repair saves line 7, but then the alphabet window of table 1 says nothing about the word.

- **Verdict: REJECT the value 138 = cattolico for the key. REJECT the statement "it is 138". Print the group as

## D. Controls on the audit itself

1. **Before the second reader's report.** The auditor wrote its own view before it opened the report. This was
   not blind: the task text named the three changes and the main evidence. The view then: change 1,
   Lesdiguières the better candidate if the name form and the fear in Rome are attested, else print both;
   change 2, a guess at most; change 3, accept as probable if the writer has "fosse" and not "fusse".
2. **After.** Change 1 went up from "better, if" to "accept, probable". The cause is not the second reader's
   argument. It is the text of the dispatches of 19 and 26 December and of 2 and 9 January, which the second
   reader did not quote, and the letters with "la Dighiera, la quale" and "la Dighiera heretica". Change 2
   stayed where it was. Change 3 stayed; the test "fosse or fusse" passed on one witness.
3. **A "blind weight" is not counted.** The second reader gave his own guess a blind weight of 70. His brief
   named Lesdiguières as an option, and he says so.
4. **Same family.** The auditor is a model of the same family as the readers. A person who knows the Roman
   letters of 1592 should check the result.
5. **What was not opened, or not done.**
   - Paruta, *La legazione di Roma*, vol. I, 1887: not opened. The words are checked in the printing of 1871; the
     page numbers of the edition are not.
   - HathiTrust: not tried. The other dispatches of Paruta of these weeks: not read.

Points under the table:

- **Why Lesdiguières and not the Queen of England.** The sheet gives [.176] an army "much feared" in a place, and
  says that with it Navarre could take Milan "very easily". England had no army near Italy. Lesdiguières took

  heresies" with his soldiers. The clear letter of the same day says that Milan had sent horse to help Savoy.

  d'Inghilterra" in another cipher letter. She fails on the army and on Milan.

  Paruta in a printing of 1871 (A. Bartoli, *Nuova antologia*, pp. 102-116), not in the edition of 1887.

### E.3 One sentence for the summary of the January sheet

> If Navarre and [name 176] (probably Lesdiguières, whose army stood in Piedmont) were [Catholics?], they could hope
> to raise the State of Milan, very ill content with Spain, and to take it with ease, because [name 176] has an army
