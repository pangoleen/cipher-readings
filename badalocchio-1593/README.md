# Vincenzo Badalocchio to the Duke of Nevers, Rome, December 1592 to February 1593: three cipher letters read

Vincenzo Badalocchio was the agent in Rome of Ludovico Gonzaga, Duke of Nevers. In the winter of 1592 to 1593 he
put the secret parts of his letters in a cipher of figures. This folder gives readings of three of these letters,
with gaps, and the one key that reads them.

## What is new

**These are the first readings known to us of the three letters. One key reads all three. It was rebuilt from
the texts; no key sheet of this cipher is known.**

- **f. 85r, 5 December 1592.** The cipher parts of the letter are read, with gaps. It is the first reading known
  to us. The key is the key of the sheet of 9 January 1593, with a second table of words that this letter showed.
  361 of the 383 cipher groups that carry text (94 %, a hand count) stand in words that we translate. All 11
  cipher runs join the clear words around them. Not read: the seven name groups (five names; we give numbers, and
  guesses with grades), one word in line 11, one group in line 12, the verb of line 13, three short doubts, and
  the groups 199 and 299 (seven places). No period decipherment of this letter is known.
- **f. 65r, 11 December 1592.** Two short cipher runs read; both complete their clear sentences; one name is not
  read.
- **f. 80r, 9(?) January 1593.** The sheet is read for about nine tenths, with gaps. This replaces our first
  reading of the same day, which had the old cut.
- "Known to us" has limits. The searches are listed under "Earlier work", with what we did not open.
- Two period decipherments exist for **other** cipher letters of Badalocchio of the same weeks. We found them
  after the first reading. They were not used to build the key. They are not decipherments of these three letters.

## The cipher

The cipher text is a row of figures with no gaps. On f. 85r and f. 65r the rows stand between clear words.

- **A figure 4, 8 or 9 takes the next figure.** The pair is one letter: 43 c, 44 u, 45 a, 46 e, 47 i, 48 e, 49 o;
  83 b, 84 c, 85 d, 86 f, 87 g, 88 h, 89 l; 93 m, 94 n, 95 p, 96 r, 97 s, 98 t, 99 q.
- **A figure 3, 5, 6 or 7 stands alone.** It is a vowel: a, i, o, u. A sign like a triangle is the letter z.
- **The figure 0 stands alone and is a null.** Six signs are nulls too. A null often stands between the two
  halves of a doubled sound: ap·p·artenersi, sareb··bono, ef··ficaci.
- **A group that starts with 1 has three figures: a word of table 1.** The table is in the order of the alphabet:
  125 al, 129 anno, 135 ben, 141 che ... 197 lui.
- **A group that starts with 2 has three figures: a word of table 2.** This table goes on in the alphabet where
  table 1 stops: 212 ma, 214 mi, 219 modo ... 286 vogli. Four endings follow: 289 endo, 291 ente, 294 re, 296 to.
- **A name is the dotted stroke and three figures 1xy:** .143, .144, .195. The key has no value for any name.
  Two places of f. 80r have only two figures after the stroke (.38, .78).
- A number in clear stands in a drawn frame (f. 80r). u and v are one letter.
- The writer uses the word groups as parts of words too: bel-LE, LA-sciata, pre-DI-ce, NE-gotio, tratta-RE,
  PER-DI-ta, SO-dis-FATTO.

**What the cut does not settle:**

- **The lone 2.** In six places a 2 cannot head a group of the tables: f. 85r lines 13 and 22; f. 80r line 7
  (three times) and line 18. The rule takes a lone 2 there. It can be a null, a group with no value, or a slip
  of the pen. This is open.
- **The cut is not the only one by form.** The three letters have 119 groups "2xy" with a value. At 74 of them
  the cut "2 = null, then letters" leaves no new group without a value. The sense settles these places: the old
  gaps close, and the measure of sense rises on each letter (see "The controls", point 5).
- **199 and 299** stand alone between clear words on f. 85r. They are groups. A null is our best guess for their
  value. It is not proved. We print them as groups with no value.

The key, with the grade of each value: [key.tsv](key.tsv). The grades are **sure** (a clear word fixes the value
in two places or more, or in one place where no other word fits), **probable** (one place, or the sense is not
complete, and the order of the alphabet allows the word) and **guess** (the place allows other words).

| Part of the key | Groups | Grades |
|---|---|---|
| Letters | 25 groups of one or two figures, and the triangle | all sure |
| Nulls | 0 and six signs | sure; one sign has one place only (probable) |
| Table 1 (1xy) | 27 words | 20 sure, 7 probable (125 al, 129 anno, 149 detta, 164 fece, 166 fin, 169 Francia, 177 ha) |
| Table 2 (2xy) | 24 words | 12 sure, 9 probable, 3 guesses (245, 246, 269) |
| Endings of table 2 | 289 endo, 291 ente, 294 re, 296 to | 2 sure, 2 probable |
| Names | 8 groups of three figures, 2 of two figures | no value in the key |

**What the key does not claim:**

- **245:** "quando" fits f. 80r line 17 ("Dio sa quando"). "quanto" fits line 16 ("di tanto valore quanto è"). One
  group cannot be both. It is open between the two.
- **246:** "quel" fits two places. In two places the sense asks for "quei" or "quelle". We take it as the stem
  "quel-".
- **269 (tanto):** one place. A guess.
- **289 (endo):** four places fit. It fails in f. 85r line 13 ("si ENDO far"). That place is not read.
- **169 (Francia):** three places fit. The fourth, after "Portogallo", has a blot and no sense.
- **199, 299 and the lone 2:** no value.

### f. 80r, 9(?) January 1593: [reading.md](reading.md)

1. The troubles of the Spanish lands in Italy make an opening. If Navarre and [name 176] were [..], they could
   hope to raise the State of Milan, very ill content with Spain, and to take it with ease. [Name 176] has an army

2. The death of [name 122] would add to this. But the heresy of the King and of [name 176] makes such fine

3. Compare the two transcription passes of each letter: `pass_a.txt` and `pass_b_blind.txt` (f. 80r), and the
   same names in `f085r/` and `f065r/`.
4. Run `python3 decode.py`. It cuts the three transcriptions, applies the key table, and prints the value of each
   group between the clear words. It counts the groups, compares the passes, and repeats the held-out test, the
   halves and the test of the order of the alphabet. It needs no other file.

The script does not make the reading. The word division, the Italian and the translation are the work of the
reader.

## The controls

Details: [evidence/audit.md](evidence/audit.md). Each control has its number and its limit.

1. **The held-out test.** The key and the cut of our first page were made on f. 80r alone. We applied them, with
   no change, to f. 85r, which they had not seen. **534 of the 569 groups (93.8 %) have a value. 313 of the 402
   groups that are not nulls (77.9 %) already have the value and the place that the final reading keeps in a
   word that we translate.** For f. 65r: 43 of 45 groups, and 27 of 39 (69.2 %). `decode.py` repeats this from
   [key_first_page.tsv](key_first_page.tsv).
   *Limits:* it is the same writer and the same cipher. The test shows that the old key was real. It does not
   test the new values. "The final reading keeps" uses the hand list of the reader.
2. **The blind audit of the key.** A fresh reader had the three transcriptions, the cut rule, and the key with
   24 values hidden. **It restored 24 of 24.**
   *Limits:* **the reader is a model of the same family as the first reader.** The two can share a habit. **The
   cut was given.** So the test does not test the cut, and it does not test the figures. **The reader used the
   order of the alphabet for 19 of the 24 values**; it fixed 5 by the sense alone. The pool holds only values with
   3 places or more: the weak values of the key are not in it. The session of the reader held a one-line index of
   the notes of the project (titles only, no key value).
3. **The order of the alphabet, all values.** The 27 words of table 1 stand in the order of the alphabet by their
   numbers. So do the 24 words of table 2. So do both as one list of 51. 0 of 20,000 shuffles are as good.
   *Limits:* the reader of f. 85r knew that table 1 was in order before it fixed any new value. Seven values of
   the second round and eleven of the first round were fixed "with the order in mind". So this form is not blind. With the four
   endings, table 2 is not in full order (rank correlation 0.598; 16 of 20,000 shuffles are as high).
4. **The order of the alphabet, strict form.** Only the values that were fixed before anybody saw the order. The
   ten words of table 1 of the first round: in order; the chance is 1 in 3.6 million. The five words that the
   first page had as groups of two figures (12 ma, 29 non, 55 se, 58 si, 66 stato): put the 2 before them, and
   they are 212, 229, 255, 258, 266, in order; the chance is 1 in 120 (152 of 20,000 shuffles). The ten and the
   five as one list: 15 of 15 in order, 0 of 20,000 shuffles.
   *Limits:* five values in order is a weak test by itself. The strength is in the list of 15, and in the fact
   that nobody knew of a second table when the five were fixed.
5. **The cut rule against rival rules.** Figures left over or forced into a group with no value: on f. 85r the
   old rule leaves 60, the new rule 2; on f. 65r 3 and 0; on f. 80r 44 and 28. The share of the letters that
   stand inside dictionary words of four letters or more rises from 56 to 72 % (f. 85r), from 37 to 54 % (f. 65r)
   and from 58 to 66 % (f. 80r). Real Italian of this writer gives 67 to 72 % by the same measure.
   *Limits:* two rival rules for the lone 2 score as well as ours (71.5 and 70.1 % on f. 85r). The measure cannot
   tell them apart. The word list is not in this repository.
6. **Shuffled keys.** The four-letter score of the cipher runs of f. 85r and f. 65r is -4.148 on 510 windows.
   Real Italian scores -4.14 in the median. 2,000 keys with shuffled values score -5.637 in the mean and -5.072
   at best. The key is 8.5 standard deviations above the mean. The true key on 2,000 shuffled orders of the
   groups: mean -5.253, best -5.025; the true order is 17.5 standard deviations above. For f. 80r the audit gives
   -4.01 with the new cut and -4.16 with the old one.
   *Limits:* the table of Italian four-letter groups is not in this repository. `decode.py` does not repeat the
   scores. The scores of our first page (-4.18; 8.3 and 21.2 standard deviations) belong to the old cut.
7. **The halves of f. 85r.** The letters, the words of the first page and the new values that stand in lines 5 to
   13 give 279 of the 289 groups of lines 14 to 25 and of f. 65r (96.5 %). The reverse gives 246 of 270 (91.1 %).
   *Limits:* the key was made on the whole page. "Stands in a half" means only that the group occurs there.
8. **The cipher runs that complete their clear sentences.** f. 85r has 11 cipher runs between clear words, and
   f. 65r has 2. **13 of 13 join the clear words at both ends. 11 make a full sentence** (the names stay numbers).
   2 do not: "e o s t i" in line 11, and the run that ends with 169 in line 12. Three of the 11 have a doubt
   inside. With the key and the cut of the first page: 0 of 13 complete.
   *Limits:* "a full sentence" is the judgement of the reader. The clear words are one reading from tiles.
9. **Two transcription passes for each letter.** f. 85r: pass A has 949 figures and signs, pass B (blind) has 920;
   906 are the same (97.0 %); pass B and the settled text agree in 916 (99.6 %). f. 65r: 72 of 72. f. 80r: 1,561
   of 1,595 places (97.9 %).
   *Limits:* pass A was not blind to the sense on any letter. Pass A of f. 85r has 29 figures too many. On
   f. 85r four places of the settled text differ from the blind pass, and the sense guided the choice in at least
   two of them.
10. **The facts, against the same writer.** Two period decipherments of other cipher letters of Badalocchio, and
    his clear letter of 9 January, treat the same matter in the same words. f. 80r has "persevera nella sua
    heresia fin al quarto anno"; the decipherment of 11 December has "Volge il quarto anno" and "persevera
    heretico". f. 85r has the Pope's demand "che ella si ritirasse dal Navara"; the decipherment of 6 February has
    "me rispose che lasci Navara", and Badalocchio's answer "quello ch'altra volta gli ho detto".
    *Limits:* these are not decipherments of our letters. They support the sense. They do not test single groups.
11. **The facts, against printed history.** Pages as the audit gives them:
    - Cardinal Gondi was stopped on his way to Rome in October 1592, under a Spanish threat: L. von Pastor,
      *History of the Popes*, vol. XXIII, pp. 67-68; H. de L'Epinois, *La Ligue et les papes* (1886), p. 573.
    - Spain pressed in Rome for the election of the Infanta in October 1592: L'Epinois, p. 574. Philip II
      proposed his daughter as Queen of France in 1593: Pastor, p. 71.
    - The four ways that were weighed in Rome in October 1592 (Navarre converted, another Bourbon prince, the
      Infanta, a prince of Lorraine): L'Epinois, p. 574.
    - The Pope was in France with the legate Cardinal Alessandrino in 1571: Pastor, p. 22.
    - The Pope sets Navarre beside the Queen of England at the end of January 1593: Pastor, p. 68.
    - Henri IV was king from 2 August 1589. January 1593 is in his fourth year.
    *Limits:* **no fact of the three readings is against the books.** But three facts stand only on the writer's
    own clear letter: Milan ill content, the King of Spain near death, and the coming of Cardinal Sforza. No book
    that we read names a mission of Sforza in these weeks.
12. **What was not found.** **Capilupi's proposal** (f. 85r line 25) is not in the decipherments and not in the
    books. **The prophecy of Cardano** (f. 80r line 10) is not identified; the reading of the words is sure.

The first blind audit of 11 October (10 of 10 hidden values restored) had the old cut. Its summary is in
[evidence/blind_audit.md](evidence/blind_audit.md).

  already gave 77.9 % of the final values of f. 85r. It was wrong in the cut of every group that starts with 2.

## Earlier work

Searched on 11 October 2026. We found no reading of f. 85r, f. 65r or f. 80r by another hand.

- **G. Cassiani, 2014 and 2016.** His two articles describe Badalocchio's work in Rome and the letters of Tommaso
  Bozio and the Duke (the references are in [nevers-1593](../nevers-1593/)). Of fr. 4700 he cites f. 67r, f. 72r-v
  (the minute), ff. 89r-v and 100r (Capilupi), and f. 109r-v. **He does not cite f. 85, f. 65 or ff. 79-80.** He
  quotes no cipher and no reading of cipher.
- **How f. 85r stands to f. 67r.** G. Cassiani cites the clear letter of 5 December 1592 on f. 67r, for its news
  of Tarugi and of the archbishopric of Avignon. f. 85r bears the same date. By our inventory they are **two
  letters of the same day**: f. 67 is one leaf, clear, about 14 lines, with its own date line and its own address
  on the back; ff. 85-86 are a separate letter, with its own date line, its signature, and its address on
  f. 86v. The news of Tarugi is on f. 67r. The cipher is on f. 85r only. Both letters name Cardinal Alessandrino:
  by Cassiani (2014, p. 395, note 44), f. 67r says that Tarugi was in France with him; on f. 85r the Pope says
  the same of himself. The inventory is one reading from small images.
- **Period decipherments.** Two exist, for other cipher letters of Badalocchio: BnF fr. 3982 no. 96,
  ff. 194r-195r (11 December 1592), and fr. 3983 no. 35, ff. 74r-75r (6 February 1593). They are in the hand of a
  clerk. We found them after our first reading. The readings were made without them. They are not decipherments
  of f. 80r, f. 85r, or the runs of f. 65r and f. 83r: for these we found none.
  See [evidence/period_decipherments.md](evidence/period_decipherments.md).
- **D. Bourdeau.** His [cyphersolver](https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver) has one line that lists fr. 4700
  nos. 38-46 among the letters to do. It has no reading of them. We copied nothing from it.
- **S. Tomokiyo** described the cipher keys of the Duke of Nevers in BnF fr. 3995
  ([Cryptiana, "Ciphers of the Duke of Nevers"](https://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/nevers.htm)). The older key of
  Badalocchio is his no. 56. A note on the back of that key says that it was replaced in September 1592. The
  folder [nevers-1593](../nevers-1593/) gives the note and the reasons why the cipher of 1593 is another one.
  His pages, as far as we had them saved, do not name Badalocchio's letters of fr. 4700.
- `NoAutopilot/cipher-lab`: no work on fr. 4700.
