# The Duke of Savoy's memorial to Philip II on the march of 3,000 Spaniards to Flanders (1573), in Vargas Mexía's Cipher 2

BnF, Espagnol 132, f.273r-274v (art. 124). Gallica `btv1b10032556x`, canvases 270 (right), 271, 272 (left).
BnF record: "chiffre de Philippe II pour Juan de Vargas Mexia". Tomokiyo's list: "Philip II to Juan de Vargas Mexia (Cipher 2)".

**What it is.** The whole piece is in cipher (116 lines, about 2,500 groups). It is not a letter. The heading and the docket say
(in cipher, in Spanish): *copy of the paper that Monsieur de la Cruz gave to His Majesty for the [42 = Duke of Savoy]*. The text
is **Italian**. The plain-hand notes are "dupp.do" (f.273r, margin) and "dupp.do / Para embiar a Juan de Vargas Mexia" (f.274v).

**Date.** No date on the piece. The text speaks of the march that the "Commendator maggiore" (Luis de Requesens, then governor
of Milan) must now make to Flanders, of the Duke of Alba as still in the Netherlands, of the French king's troops going towards
"the camp of La Rochelle", and of terms offered to La Rochelle and Nîmes. That is **spring or early summer 1573**. Juan de Vargas
Mexía was then Philip II's ambassador in Turin. So the piece is five years older than the Paris letters, and "Cipher 2" was
already his cipher in 1573. (My inference from the text; no outside source seen for the date.)

**Status.** First reading with the known key, as far as I can find (key: S. Tomokiyo 2020). No decipherment on the leaves.
Not searched: Archivio di Stato di Torino, AGS Estado (Saboya, Milán), printed Savoy correspondence.

**Passes and rates.** Two blind agents per page (sign names and line tiles only): agreement of groups f.273r 640/778 = 82.3 %,
f.273v 609/698 = 87.2 %, f.274r 610/702 = 86.9 %, f.274v 299/345 = 86.7 %. The text below follows pass A and takes pass B where A
gives no word. Wrong-key control (Italian trigram model, 500 shuffled keys): 0/500 as good on every page (numbers in NOTES.md).
The word division, the capitals, the punctuation and the double letters are mine: the cipher writes single consonants
("pasagio", "deto", "tuto"). [ ] = hidden in the binding or restored; (?) = doubtful.

## Heading (f.273r, lines 1-2; Spanish)

## Text (Italian)

**f.273r.** Il Signor Duca di Savoia mio signore, mosso dal continuo zelo che ha del servitio di Vostra Maestà, et anco per non
mancare a quello che gli pare convenir a se stesso, all'obligo grande(?) che le ha, et al beneficio publico, non ostante che non sia
stato richiesto da alcuno di dar parere né consiglio nell'impresa di Fiandra et nel passaggio che devono hora far gli tre milla
Spagnuoli, ha preso ardire, per la pratica che ha di quei paesi, essendo[ne] stato governatore, et per qualche cognitione che Dio
gli ha dato delle cose di guerra, raccordare a Vostra Maestà alcune cose che gli paiono degne di consideratione in questo negotio,
et insieme avvertirla di qualche avisi havuti circa detto passaggio; supplicandola che resti servita di accettar il tutto in buona
parte, et assicurarsi che ciò procede solamente dal gran zelo che esso Signor Duca ha di vedere le cose di Vostra Maestà ridotte a
segno di potersi presto ben finire, et che piglino quello esito che ella può desiderare per sodisfatione et quiete sua.

Prima, risolvendo[si] Vostra Maestà di far passar li detti Spagnuoli in Fiandra per il camino che fece il Signor Duca d'Alva, deve
considerare che quel viaggio (come lei sa) è assai longo, et dove occorresi far con sì poca gente (con tutto che vada in volta

debba passare con detti Spagnuoli), resta molto soggetto a ricever danno in alcuni passi che vi sono, **(f.273v)** li quali
nell'andata del Signor Duca d'Alva [fu]rno(?) meglio considerati et intesi da quelli che si [sa]rebbero potuti opporre al detto
passaggio; et ho[ra], sapendo che detto Signor Commendatore maggiore [o] altri d'ordine di Vostra Maestà debba passar con menor
numero di gente, et forsi non t[an]to buona, potrebbero per ventura perdere le v[er]gogna, onde ne succederebbe qualche
inconv[e]niente.

Oltre di questo, havendo il Re [di] Francia [fat]to caminare le sue genti d'armi con a[l]cune fanterie che haveva nel Delfinato
a[lla] volta di Lenguadoche, et indi per ventura [al] campo della Rochiela, s'intende che gli Ugo[not]ti di quel paese cominciano
a riarmarsi, pare[n]dogli in certo modo di esser restati in lib[er]tà. Hora, intendendo questo passaggio, potrebb[e]ro facilmente
concertarsi con quelli di Geneva et loro vicini et fare qualche movimen[to]; tanto più havendo il Signor Duca d'Alva [le]vato alla
Borgogna alcune compagnie de[lla] miglior gente che vi fosse. Per il che dev[e] Vostra Maestà havere al detto passaggio tanto
[mag]gior consideratione et riguardo, come [..] pa[ri]mente detto Signor Duca ne ha avvertit[o] il Signor Commendator maggiore; [et]
deve tanto [più] farlo Vostra Maestà per l'aviso che si h[a] di buon luogo, come il [du = Re di Francia] è consigliato da mo[l]ti
delli suoi di far qualsivoglia partit[o a] quelli della Rochiela, a quelli di Nimes et [al]tri ribelli di suoi stati, per
acquieta[re] il suo Regno, vedendo le cose del mondo ca[mi]nar di sorte che, stando lui pacifico nel s[uo] **(f.274r)** Regno, se
gli potrebbe presentar occasione di accrescerlo et ampliarlo, come più largamente ne ho dato conto a bocca a Vostra Maestà; il che
mi scuserà di non esser quivi prolisso.

Laonde è di parere detto Signor Duca che, risolvendosi Vostra Maestà di far passar detta gente in Fiandra, ne dia aviso al [du] et
alli Signori Svizzeri, per levargli ogni sospetto et gelosia, con assigurargli che questo non è per tornargli in danno alcuno, ma

raccomanda, pregando Iddi[o] che felicissima per infiniti anni la conse[rvi].

## English

*Copy of the paper that Monsieur de la Cruz(?) gave to His Majesty for the [Duke of Savoy].*

The Duke of Savoy my lord is moved by his constant zeal for Your Majesty's service, and also by what he thinks he owes to himself,
to his great debt to you and to the common good. Nobody has asked him for an opinion or advice on the enterprise of Flanders or
on the passage that the three thousand Spaniards must now make. Even so he makes bold to recall some points to Your Majesty,
because he knows those countries (he was their governor) and because God has given him some knowledge of war; and to warn you of
some news that he has about that passage. He begs you to take all in good part and to be sure that it comes only from his great
wish to see Your Majesty's affairs soon brought to a good end, with the outcome that you can wish for your content and peace.

First. If Your Majesty decides to send those Spaniards to Flanders by the road that the Duke of Alba took, consider that the
journey, as you know, is very long. With so few men it is exposed to harm at some passes on the way. (The reports do say that the
Italian troops now raised in Lombardy, and the Comendador Mayor in person, are to pass with those Spaniards.) When the Duke of Alba
went, those who could have opposed the passage weighed those passes with more respect. Now they know that the Comendador Mayor, or
others by your order, must pass with fewer men, and perhaps not such good ones; they could lose their shame, and some mischief
could follow.

Further. The King of France has marched his men-at-arms and some foot that he had in Dauphiné towards Languedoc, and from there
perhaps to the camp of La Rochelle. It is heard that the Huguenots of that country begin to arm again, as they think themselves in
a way left free. If they hear of this passage, they could easily agree with those of Geneva and their neighbours and make some
move; the more so because the Duke of Alba has taken from Burgundy some companies of the best men there. So Your Majesty must give
this passage all the more thought and care, as the Duke has also warned the Comendador Mayor. There is one more reason: good
sources report that many of his own advise the King of France to grant any terms to those of La Rochelle, of Nîmes and other
rebels of his states, to quiet his kingdom. They see that the affairs of the world go in such a way that, if he is at peace at
home, a chance could come to him to enlarge his kingdom. I have told Your Majesty more of this by word of mouth, so I do not write
at length here.

The Duke therefore thinks that, if Your Majesty decides to send those men to Flanders, you should give notice to the King of
France and to the Swiss lords, to take away all suspicion and jealousy, with the assurance that this is not meant to harm them,
only to take those men to Flanders for your service. The Duke offers to do the same on his side and to remove any shadow or bad
impression that they could have.

Last, may it please Your Majesty to order the Comendador Mayor to provide the Duke with the money needed to raise more men(?) and
to put them in the castles and passes of his states, as was done when the Duke of Alba passed, so that no ill-willed spirit gets
there first, to the harm of Your Majesty's service and of the Duke.

So the Duke thinks that, for all these reasons and many others left out for shortness, Your Majesty must make every effort, lose
no time and spare no cost or labour, to finish the affairs of Flanders. For this he holds that one of the most important and
necessary things for your service in those countries is to stay master of the sea: for the victuals, for trade, and for the help
from Spain. In the end, he who stays master of the sea will also be lord of the land. Your Majesty should therefore order that
this be the first aim and that every effort be made in it; you have good means to do it by way of Flanders and by way of Biscay
with its neighbours.

This is what the Duke has to recall to Your Majesty for now. He commends himself humbly to your good grace and prays God to keep
you most happy for countless years.

## Points that are not settled

- "Mos de la Cruz": the cipher has `mo-s de-la cru-[z?]`; the last sign is read as the z sign by me, `9,` by pass B and `p?` by
  pass A. The Savoy envoy is not identified. "Monsieur de la Croix" is a guess.
- `[42]` = the Duke of Savoy: from the heading against the first words of the text; one piece only.
- `[du]` = the King of France: f.273v L10 spells "il Re [di] Francia" and L28 and f.274r L7 use `du` for the same person.
- f.273r L6 "obligo grande" (`pl-a-n-de`), L12 "essendo[ne] stato governatore" (`esendo gli stato gouernatore`), L29 "occorresi far",
  L32 "Ill.mo" (a sign with a cross above); f.273v L1 "[fu]rno", L7-8 "le v[er]gogna"; f.274r L19 "accrescer gente" (`ge-o-a-le`).
- 27 line ends of f.273v and all 16 of f.274v run into the binding; one or two groups can be lost at each.
