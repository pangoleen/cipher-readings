# BnF Espagnol 318 no. 93: a letter in cipher, Venice, 24 February 1504

Reading of 9 October 2026, corrected after the audit of 11 October 2026.
By the catalogue, the writer is Lorenzo Suárez de Figueroa, Spanish ambassador at Venice, and the letter goes to
Ferdinand and Isabella. The page has no signature and no address.

This is the first reading known to us. The key is rebuilt from the letter. We found no period decipherment (the
searches are listed in the notes of the folder). The work was done by a model. No palaeographer has checked it.

## Conventions

- One row = one line of the page. `r` = f. 118 recto (33 lines), `v` = f. 118 verso (17 lines).
- The Spanish follows the key, token by token. The machine decode with no editing is in the folder.
- Word division, capitals, accents and punctuation are ours. We keep the spelling of the cipher (*enbiaron*,
  *cunplir*, *Sesena*, *tegua*).
- `(?)` after a word = the value is a guess or is doubtful (grade C in the key table).
- `[xxx]` = a code word with no value. `[..]` in the English = a gap. We leave a gap as a gap.
- A letter in square brackets, as in *un[a]*, is not on the page. We add it.
- A hyphen at the end of a row = the word goes on in the next line of the page.
- *Italic* in the last row = clear Spanish on the page.
- The English follows the Spanish line by line. So the English word order is not always natural.

## Notes on single lines

- **r01.** The line begins with a cross and a pause mark. The cross can be the usual cross at the head of a letter.
  Then "ayer" has no sign for its "a". The code word after "letra" is `gie` (como). The sense asks for "del"
  (`gie~`). We see no stroke over the word on the image. So "[del]" is ours.
- **r01-r02.** "embaxador", "entre" and "rey de Francia" stand once each. The diary of Sanuto gives the same news
  for the same days (the notes of the folder have the columns). So the sense is settled. The exact words stay open.
- **r03 and r17.** The sign `tb` has the value d. It does not fit these two words: "posedendo" should be
  "poseyendo", and "ynterpudsiese" should be "ynterpusiese". The sign in r17 is under a blot.
- **r05 and r17.** "para" is the value that the audit gave to `noe`. The first reading had "a".
- **r07.** "el alegría" can be "su alegría". The code word `pot` stands once, and its value is doubtful.
- **r08-r10.** Not settled. The words "do no dar", "vi" and "[..]cre" give no sure sense. One sign in r10 has no
  value.
- **r13-r14.** Not settled. Two code words have no value. "ya" does not fit after "veen".
- **r15.** "mal" is a guess of the audit. One code word has no value.
- **r18-r21.** The letter does not name who "have had" the castles of Cesena. By Sanuto, the Pope's side got them
  in these days. Who "wished to settle" is open. In r18-r19 the Spanish has "ni" (nor) with no "no" before it, and
  a strong pause mark stands after "restituýr". The sense is: the two sides cannot agree over Faenza and Rimini.
- **r28-r29.** "servicio" is a guess of the audit.
- **r29-r31.** The audit corrected this sentence. The sign `ir` is "o" in all its four places. So the text is
  "o me hagan medianero o no". The ambassador means to keep the Pope and Venice apart.
- **r33.** After "juntarse" the page has the letters "os". A short word is missing or is not written.
- **r15, r24, r32, r33, v11, v12.** The audit swapped two code words. `ren` is "Venecianos" and `hog` is "estos".
  In v11-v12 the first reading had "Venecianos desdeños se muestran por estos". This is not Spanish. The corrected
  text is "Estos desdeños se muestran por Venecianos".
- **v01.** Three code words have no value.
- **v06-v07.** Doubtful. "embaxada" stands once and is a guess. The verb "venían" is plural. One sign after
  "escrito" is not sure (we read it as a second "o"). By Sanuto, an envoy of the Sultan was in Venice on 6 March 1504. So the embassy was not
  put off for long. Either the ambassador reports a false rumour, or our words are wrong.
- **v07 and v11.** "piensan" is doubtful. The alphabet rule asks for a word in c or d here ("creen", "dizen").
- **v14.** Both blind readers of the audit gave "verdad" for `roo` ("and in truth it is a very great business").
  The decoder does not use this guess.
- **v15.** The x of "existencia" is a sign that stands once. Its value is a guess.
- **v16.** By Zurita and Sanuto, Gonzalo Fernández was gravely ill in January and February 1504. News that he is
  well fits. The two code words stand for the sense. The exact words are guesses.

## Summary

The ambassador reports how Venice took the news of the truce of three years between Spain and France. Venice
showed pleasure. He hid his own view. Venice and the Pope are in a quarrel over Faenza and Rimini, and they talk
of a go-between. He will accept if both sides ask him. He will not press the business before he has an order,
and he means to work so that the two stay apart: then Venice must join Spain. The Pope leans to France. Louis
d'Ars is besieged in Venosa. The Sultan's embassy is put off (doubtful). Venice makes light of the Portuguese
spice trade, and the people of Venice are in great confusion over it. Gonzalo Fernández is well again (the sense
is supported, the words are guesses).
